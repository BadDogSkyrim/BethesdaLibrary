    Starfield: Character Generation, Race, Skeleton & Body

    Everything the Starfield engine needs to build, morph, tint, skin, and bake a playable character — with an eye toward what a custom anthropomorphic (furry) playable race would require. Starfield's chargen is a direct descendant of the Fallout 4 / Skyrim system, but nearly every piece has been rebuilt: geometry lives in external .mesh files, morphs are .morph/.dat deltas, tints are a layered "AVM" material system, and animated appendages can be driven by Bone Modifiers (BMOD) attached at the race level instead of a hand-authored Havok behavior graph.

    Record field names below come from the SF1Edit (xEdit) Starfield record definitions (wbDefinitionsSF1.pas), cross-checked against Starfield.esm. Mesh/skeleton details come from nifly's BSGeometry implementation and community tooling (Starfield Geometry Bridge / StarfieldMeshConverter). Community-folklore claims are marked Unverified.

    1. The RACE record

    How races are used (verified against Starfield.esm)

    Starfield.esm contains just 31 RACE records, and essentially every human person in the game is the single HumanRace [0000347D]. Starfield dropped the many-races model — there is no race choice in chargen; all human variety is appearance/morphs on that one race. The only other humanoid races are HumanCorpseRace [00034FB1] (bodies), ChildRace [002AABCD], HumanCrowdRace [002BBC09] (ambient background crowds), and MannequinRace [001EE4DA]; the remaining ~26 are creatures/fauna (QuadrupedA/B/C, BipedA, FloaterA, Terrormorph…) and machines (AutopilotRace, *TurretRace, SecurityCameraRace…).

    When generating a custom race, you generally want to start by cloning HumanRace — you inherit everything the game's race checks rely on. Two possible design paths:

    • Clone HumanRace → a new race, assigned to the player and chosen NPCs. Selective, toggleable, mod-compatible (the article-431 route documented below), can share the world with humans.
    • Override HumanRace itself (swap its skin ARMO / default head parts) → everyone changes, with no per-NPC editing, but it's total, not toggleable, and conflicts with other appearance/body mods. Ambient crowds are HumanCrowdRace (override that too for full coverage); kids are ChildRace.

    Where race boundaries should fall: When you have multiple closely-related races (wolf, jackal, fox) you might make each its own race or you might make one "Canid" race with these being customizations (using Starfield's ethnicity mechanism, perhaps). HOWEVER, texture layers are not race-specific -- there's one texture stack for the entire game. So you won't be able to cleanly separate textures for the different species, and you'll have to create extra textures just to fill in all the slots. It's likely more convenient to put each species in its own race -- possibly sharing a single head with morphs for the different head shapes.

    What actually "notices" a race swap: conditions testing a keyword (ActorTypeNPC/ActorTypeHuman, etc.) pass — a clone inherits HumanRace's keywords, covering most generic AI/quest/dialogue checks. Conditions testing the specific race form (GetIsRace HumanRace) or a race form-list fail until you add your race — which is exactly what the finishing-fix form-lists patch (e.g. HumanRaceORChildRaceORHumanCorpseRace [CNDF:0003D8C1]; see §9). Changing the race of named NPCs also means re-baking FaceGen for each NPC.

    Selecting the player's race is done by plugin, not in the creator (same as [FO4]): Starfield has no in-chargen race picker, so a mod sets the player's race by overriding the player NPC record (form 7) + its 12 copies (article 431).

    Ambient crowds (HumanCrowdRace) — generated on the fly

    Background city crowds are not hand-authored NPCs with baked FaceGen — they use a separate, deliberately lightweight race (HumanCrowdRace [002BBC09]) and a data-driven spawn system:

    • The crowd race has its own crowd body skin (Skin_Crowd_Base_Body_NOTPLAYABLE) and its own skeleton (actors\human_crowd\…\skeleton.nif) but shares human animations/ rig. Crucially it carries almost no chargen data — no race presets, no morph groups, no race-level head-part list (just a "default" phenotype + a skin index).
    • Crowds draw on per-location leveled lists: LCharCrowdKeywords_DEFAULT (+ _Akila / _NewAtlantisWell / _NeonUnderbelly) is a list of ~58 template NPCs named by mood × prop (CrowdActor_KEYWORDS_Angry_Briefcase, _Angry_Coffee…), fed into the actors' Keywords template slot; LCharCrowdVoices_Male/Female list voice templates. (The engine also LOD-renders distant ambient walkers on top.)
    • The crowd actor records carry no face — but the face is not engine-generated from scratch; it's a randomly-equipped head ARMO. Verified across the 181 CrowdActor_* NPC records: each has RNAM=HumanCrowdRace, WNAM=crowd body skin, a skin-tone index, a randomized height range (e.g. 0.98–1.02), an outfit and a voice — but no PNAM head parts, no face morphs, EDCT (tint count) = 0. Most (95 of 138) inherit only Factions and Keywords, but 24 also inherit Traits from the voice lists, and Traits carries race (crowds §2). The head instead comes from the outfit: each Outfit_Crowds_* equips clothing plus a leveled list LL_Crowd_Heads [000F4E92] (variants _Overweight / _Thin) that draws one of 8 authored CrowdHead_<phenotype> ARMOs (CrowdHead_af_md1, _eu_yo1, … ethnicity×age). A CrowdHead ARMO sits on biped slot 2 (Hide Head) and shows its head mesh via an AA_CrowdHead_* addon. So a crowd face = a random head ARMO from a finite pool + random height. No per-NPC FaceGen, and no skin tone on the head: each crowd head is one mesh (head, eyes and arms) with its own baked textures (crowds §3).

    Full crowd appearance pipeline: 

    CrowdActor NPC → DOFT: Outfit_Crowds_<location>
                    ├─ clothing items
                    └─ LL_Crowd_Heads (leveled list, 8 CrowdHead_* ARMOs)
                          └─ random CrowdHead_<phenotype>  → ARMO on slot 2 (Hide Head)
                                └─ AA_CrowdHead_* addon → head mesh
  body: WNAM = Skin_Crowd_Base_Body   ·   skin tone: STON   ·   height: randomized

    Modding implication (appearance): the head is an equippable ARMO chosen by a leveled list, so a crowd of another species is a data job, not per-NPC FaceGen. What worked in game for a fox crowd (October 2026): its own crowd race, new NPCs copied from crowd actors (with inherited Traits turned off), and fox head armors on a new head list in those NPCs' outfits, leaving every vanilla record alone. Because the head carries the arms and hands, the head is the only part that needs to show species; a crowd outfit is one full-body garment, a head and a hair-or-hat. The details, including the _facebones copy, the BoneTrn eye positions and the open question of tails, are on the crowds page.

    Record fields

    Signature RACE. Far larger than its predecessors. The parts that matter for a new race:

    Core data (DAT2 "Data" struct)

    Field Meaning
    Male Height / Female Height scale multipliers
    Male/Female Default Weight three floats each: Thin / Muscular / Fat — the body-triangle anchors
    Head Biped Object biped slot index that counts as "head"
    Hair Biped Object slot index for hair
    Beard Biped Object slot index for beard
    Body Biped Object slot index for body
    Shield Biped Object / Pipboy Biped Object slot indices
    Flags (u64), Intelligence Level AI/behavior
    OnCripple / Bleedout / Electromagnetic Shocked combat-dismemberment reactions

    WNAM Skin → ARMO (the default skin armor — the body/hands mesh set). GNAM Body Part Data → BPTD (dismemberment regions; see the dismemberment.md page for the FO4/Skyrim ancestor).

    Skeleton Data (per gender)

    Skeleton Data
├─ Male Data   (MNAM marker)
│   ├─ ANAM  Skeletal Model     — path to the skeleton .nif
│   ├─ NAM5  Skeleton Rig
│   ├─ NAM6  Animation Root
│   └─ DNAM  Animations
└─ Female Data (FNAM marker)    — same fields
    This is where a custom-skeleton race points at its own skeleton.nif, rig, and animation root. Contrast [Skyrim]/[FO4] which used a single ANAM Skeletal Model string per gender with far less structure.

    FaceGen clamps

    • PNAM FaceGen – Main clamp
    • UNAM FaceGen – Face clamp

    These bound how far the chargen morphs may push the face.

    Biped Object Names / Conditions

    • NAME × 64 — human-readable names for the 64 biped slots.
    • RBPC × 64 — a per-slot condition (AVIF) gating whether the slot shows.

    Compare [FO4] 32 biped slots / [Skyrim] 32 body-part flags — Starfield doubles it to 64 and splits armor vs. spacesuit ("SS") variants (see §7).

    Chargen and Skintones (per gender) — the character-creator payload

    Phenotypes: Much of character customization depends on "phenotypes", a new concept in Starfield. A phenotype describes a basic look for a head. Starfield phenotype names follow the form <sex>_<ethnicity>_<age>, where "sex" = male/female, "ethnicity" = af (african)/as (asian)/eu (european), and "age" = md (middle aged)/ol (old)/yo (young). That's 18 variants jut to cover the ground; variation within phenotype is in addition to that. Phenotypes can control both facial morphs and textures.

    Chargen and Skintones
└─ Male / Female
   ├─ Chargen (NAM0 marker)
   │   ├─ Race Presets      RPRM/RPRF → NPC_  (preset faces in the creator)
   │   ├─ Morph Groups      MPGN name + MPGM morph-name list
   │   ├─ Face Morph Phenotypes  FMRI index + FMRN name  (named face presets)
   │   ├─ Skin Tone AVMS Subtype (FDDS string)
   │   └─ Face Dials        FDSI Skin Index + FDSL Label  (individual sliders)
   ├─ Body Skin Tones   (BSTT string path)
   ├─ Hand Skin Tones   (HSTT string path)
   ├─ Face Custom Textures Base Path  (FCTP)
   └─ Face Skin Tones   (FSTT string path)
    So the creator UI is data-driven from the RACE record: the list of sliders ("Face Dials"), the named presets ("Face Morph Phenotypes"), the grouped morphs ("Morph Groups"), and the skin-tone palettes are all here. There is no separate "genes" record type — what players call the "genes" system is this combination of face dials + morph phenotypes + shape-blend morphs + skin-tone AVM subtypes.

    Field reference (what SF1Edit shows under "Chargen and Skintones")

    Record Label Meaning
    MNAM / FNAM Male Data / Female Data per-gender marker (the whole block repeats for each sex)
    NAM0 Chargen start of the chargen block
    RPRM / RPRF Race Presets (male/female) → NPC_ the preset faces scrolled in the creator; each points to an NPC_ defining a full face
    MPGN + MPGM Morph Groups MPGN = group name = a face region (Cheeks, Chin, Ears, Eyes, Forehead, Jaw, Mouth, Neck, Nose); MPGM = the list of morph-key names in that region — one per phenotype (e.g. male_af_md1_Cheeks, male_eu_md2_Cheeks, …). These are the creator's per-region sliders; the names are the keys in the chargen morph.dat (§3).
    FMRI + FMRU/FMRN/FMRS Face Morph Phenotypes the named base faces: ethnicity (af/as/eu) × age (yo/md/ol) = 9 (male_af_md1, male_eu_md2, female_as_yo1, …). FMRU and FMRS are the raw name; FMRN is the localized display name (a string ID in Starfield.esm, literal text in an unlocalized plugin). FMRI is not a positional index — it is the ID of a region in the race's FacialBoneRegions<Sex>.txt, so the values are sparse (male: 1, 9, 12, 14, 16, 40, 42, 44, 46).
    FMSR + FMRU/FMRN sculpt regions interleaved into the same numbered table as FMRI, these are the 11 SculptRegion: true regions — Head Shapes, Cheeks, Chin, Ears, Eyebrows, Eyes, Forehead, Jaw, Mouth, Neck, Nose (male IDs 22, 26, 30, 34, 38, 50, 56, 62, 66, 72, 82). Both kinds of row are entries in one flat table whose IDs run in increasing order. NOTE, As of this writing, xEdit shows these nested under FMRI -- it's clearer to think of them as at the same level, interleaved.
    FDDS Skin Tone AVMS Subtype presumably names the AVM schema (§4) the skin tones conform to — but neither HumanRace nor any shipped custom race sets it, so the purpose is unconfirmed.
    FDSI + FDSL Face Dials the individual named sliders in the creator. ⚠ xEdit labels FDSI "Skin Index" and it is nothing to do with skin tones — it is a sculpt-slider ID, the same space the NPC's FMSI indexes. HumanRace carries 40 dials per sex, sparse (24, 25, 27, 28, 29, 31, … 85), and 24/25/76/77 are exactly the sliders the Mouth region (ID 22) owns — see §3. FDSL is the translatable label ('down - up', 'narrow - wide').
    BSTT / HSTT / FSTT Body / Hand / Face Skin Tones the AVM skin-tone group names the race draws its skin from (§4); a custom race points these at its own groups (guide 431, §"Adding skin tones")
    FCTP Face Custom Textures Base Path the directory (under Data\Textures\) the FaceGen bake reads its base face maps from, by filename convention — <phenotype>_sk<N>_color/_normal/_rough/_ao.dds plus the per-region blend masks FCT_<region>_mask.dds (§4.5). Vanilla HumanRace omits it and falls back to [Facegen] sSkinTexturePath (default Actors\Human\Faces\Chargen); FelidRace sets actors\Felid\faces\chargen. A custom race should set this — see Creation Kit §3.

    How it composes: 9 regions × 18 phenotypes = 162 chargen morphs (§3), named <phenotype>_<region> — male_as_md1_Cheeks, female_eu_yo1_Nose. Morph Groups list those names per region; Face Morph Phenotypes name the base faces the sliders blend between; Face Dials are extra individual sliders. The same phenotype vocabulary addresses two different deformation systems — the vertex morphs here, and the bone morphs in the race's FacialBoneRegions file, which uses the identical names. §3 covers how an NPC drives both.

    A custom race authors its own MPGN/MPGM + FMRI/FMRU and its own skin-tone AVM groups (BSTT/HSTT/FSTT). Keep the two halves consistent: a phenotype named X implies morph keys X_Cheeks, X_Nose and so on, and a region ID that exists in the FacialBoneRegions file.

    What the RACE record does not reach

    A custom race is not self-contained. Some of what the engine needs it finds by a hardcoded FormID or by a naming convention, never by following a link out of your RACE record. Nothing in your plugin points at these, so nothing warns you when they are wrong — the failure is always silent, and usually looks like "works in the Creation Kit, broken in game".

    Consolidated, with the symptom when each is missed:

    What Reached how Miss it and…
    HeadPartsChargenOptionsMale [FLST:0003D9AF] / …Female [0003D9B0] hardcoded FormID your head parts never appear as creator choices; the creator fits the human part to your skull
    CharGenPresets [FLST:0003F551] hardcoded FormID the creator's preset row still offers human faces (each preset's head comes from that NPC's own race)
    The player records (Player [NPC_:00000007] + its copies) hardcoded FormID you stay human where it matters — see §9 for the full list
    HumanRaceORChildRaceORHumanCorpseRace [CNDF:0003D8C1] hardcoded FormID spacesuit does not show
    DC_Race_Human [CPTH:000C1056] hardcoded FormID dialogue camera sits too close
    Clothes_GenWare_01 "Space Undersuit" [ARMO:00165722] hardcoded FormID invisible limbs in new-game chargen
    SimpleGroup_EyebrowOpacity [AVMD:0000EF9F] by name eyebrows render fully transparent
    Every AVMD tint slot (Dermaesthetic, EyeColor, …) by name (§4.4) a group you invented under a new name is simply never read; you must override the vanilla name or extend it with AVMP (§4.11)
    Chargen_* twin tint groups by name the Creation Kit looks right and the in-game creator does not — CK reads the runtime group, the creator reads the Chargen_ twin
    …FacialBoneRegions<Sex>.txt + …Mapping.csv race EDID filename face morph regions do not resolve
    BaseHead<RaceEDID><Sex>_<map>.dds race EDID filename rename the race and the old bake orphans silently → black/stale head (§8)
    The four FCTP face maps filename convention in the race's FCTP directory a missing normal renders the face black (§1 FCTP)

    ⚠ Renaming your race EDID breaks the last three at once, and none of them will tell you.

    2. Head Parts (HDPT)

    Signature HDPT. This is the single most important record for a furry race — Starfield added dedicated Creature head-part types.

    Type enum (PNAM)

    # Type # Type
    0 Misc 10 Head Rear
    1 Face 11 Extra Hair
    2 Right Eye 12 Left Eye
    3 Hair 13 Eyelashes
    4 Facial Hair 14 Creature Head
    5 Scar 15 Creature Torso
    6 Eyebrows 16 Creature Arms
    7 Jewelry 17 Creature Legs
    8 Meatcaps 18 Creature Tail
    9 Teeth 19 Creature Wings

    Types 14–19 are Bethesda's built-in hooks for non-human morphology. A muzzle/ snout would most naturally be a Creature Head (or a Face + extra part), a tail a Creature Tail, ears a Head-Rear/Extra part or a Creature part. Note the split of eyes into Right Eye / Left Eye (vs [Skyrim]/[FO4] single "Eyes"), plus new Meatcaps (gore stumps), Head Rear, Eyelashes.

    HDPT fields

    Field Meaning
    DATA Flags Playable, Male, Female, Is Extra Part, Use Solid Tint, Uses Body Texture, Hide with "HideEar" Morph
    Model .nif path (which points at an external .mesh — §5)
    HNAM Extra Parts list of sub-HDPTs pulled in together (e.g. a head that also spawns eyes)
    NAM2 Color Mapping AVMS/AVMD color-group name
    NAM3 Mask tint mask name
    TNAM Texture Set → TXST
    RNAM Valid Races → FLST (a FormID list of races the part is offered to)
    MNAM Morph → MRPH (the morphable-object record; replaces the TRI reference)

    RNAM Valid Races is the mechanism by which a new head part is offered in the creator only to your race. The Hide with "HideEar" Morph flag ties a whole head part's visibility to the Hide Ear toggle (biped slot 19), which helmets/headgear occupy — so the part vanishes when headgear is worn. Handy for a separate horn or ear part that should disappear under a helmet. (Ears integrated into the head mesh instead use the head's own HideEar morph to collapse the ear region — see §3 Expressions.)

    The .nif and the morph.dat are siblings under HDPT, not linked to each other. A head part points at its mesh through Model (meshes/…/*.nif → an external .mesh) and at its morphs through MNAM → MRPH → TMPP/TCMP (meshes/morphs/…/morph.dat). Nothing inside the NIF references the morph file: the two live in different trees (meshes/ vs meshes/morphs/), their names don't correspond, and the only thing that binds them is the shared HDPT record. So a tool importing a head NIF cannot auto-discover its morphs — the association has to come from the record (or be supplied by the user). The Creation Kit's "tri" field on a head part is this MNAM → MRPH reference (the label is a Skyrim/FO4 legacy — Starfield morphs are morph.dat, not .tri); it is used, and every vanilla Face / Eyebrows / Teeth / Eyelashes head part sets it.

    3. Morphs — MRPH record and morph.dat geometry

    [Skyrim]/[FO4] used .tri files referenced by a BODYTRI/FaceGen NiStringExtraData. Starfield replaces .tri with a MRPH "Morphable Object" record plus external morph-delta files.

    MRPH record fields

    Field Meaning
    TMPP Target Morph Path expression/animation morph file
    TCMP Target Chargen Morph Path the chargen-slider morph file
    BMPP Bone Morph Definition File drives bones from morph values (skeletal morphs)
    MOBC Base Vertex Color base color for the morphed mesh

    Key insight: a MRPH separates the performance morphs (expressions, visemes) from the chargen morphs (creator sliders). BMPP can additionally map morph sliders onto bone transforms — but note that vanilla head/body MRPH records and shipped custom races leave BMPP empty; the bone-driven face shaping actually in use is the FacialBoneRegions system described below, which is wired by file-naming convention rather than through MRPH.

    Expressions (performance morphs) — verified from vanilla assets

    Facial expressions and lip-sync are blendshape morphs, stored in a separate performance/ morph.dat from the creator sliders. The morph tree splits every head part three ways:

    meshes/morphs/human/<sex>/performance/head/morph.dat   ← runtime expressions
meshes/morphs/human/<sex>/chargen/head/morph.dat        ← creator sliders
meshes/morphs/.../chargen/facefx/morph.dat              ← chargen-time face-FX preview

    The extracted human female performance/head/morph.dat has 96 shape keys (15370 verts) — textbook FACS action units: jawOpen, jawClench, jawLeft/Right/Thrust, browLowererL/R, innerBrowRaiseL/R, outerBrowRaiseL/R, eyeClosedL/R, eyeOpenL/R, squintL/R, lidTightenerL/R, cheekPuffL/R, cheekRaiseL/R, dimplerL/R, lipCornerPullL/R, lipCornerDepressL/R, lipStretchL/R, lipPucker, lipPress, upperLipRaiseL/R, lowerLipDepressL/R, noseWrinkleL/R, nostrilDilator, chinRaise, neckFlexL/R, swallow, HideEar, plus c_-prefixed combination/corrective morphs (e.g. c_eyeLeft_eyeClosedL, c_squintL_cheekRaiserL) that fire when two action units co-activate. These action units double as visemes for lip-sync.

    By contrast the chargen/head/morph.dat (84 keys) holds the creator morphs. Its structure is exactly 3 body-shape keys + 9 phenotype presets × 9 face regions (verified on the vanilla male & female head morph.dat):

    • Body shape (3): Overweight, Thin, Strong (the same three keys as the chargen/body/morph.dat).
    • Face presets (81): <sex>_<ethnicity>_<age>_<Region>.
    • 9 phenotype presets = ethnicity af/as/eu × age yo/md/ol (young/mid/old), i.e. af_md1, af_ol1, af_yo1, as_md1, as_ol1, as_yo1, eu_md2, eu_ol1, eu_yo1. (Note eu uses md2 where af/as use md1 -- it's not clear why.)
    • 9 regions = Cheeks, Chin, Ears, Eyes, Forehead, Jaw, Mouth, Neck, Nose.

    So male_eu_md2_Cheeks, female_af_yo1_Jaw, etc. — each region exposes 9 sliders (one per preset), and the creator blends them per region. The two channels are entirely distinct sets, referenced by MRPH.TMPP (performance) vs MRPH.TCMP (chargen). A specific NPC's face is then built from these: the NPC_ record's Face Morphs array holds FMRI face-morph indices (into this preset set) plus per-region Morph Groups (FMRG name + FMRS blend intensity), and its MRSV Body Morph Region Values blend the body-shape keys.

    Each face part (head, eyebrows, eyelashes, teeth…) has its own performance/morph.dat, so the whole face animates together. The runtime in-house animation/behavior system (.agx/.af) drives which action units blend for dialogue emotion, idle emotes, and lip-sync. (The _facebones.nif rig is a separate mechanism — facial-region bone weighting for chargen sculpting; expressions themselves are morph-based, not bone-based.)

    Every attached face part carries its own performance/morph.dat holding a subset of the same named action units — not just head/eyebrows/eyelashes/teeth but every individual hairstyle (and tear meshes, hair beads, etc.). Sizes scale to what each part needs: the head has 96 keys, an eyebrow 54, a tear mesh 73, and a hair mesh only ~15 (the big jawOpen/jawThrust/neckFlex/browLowerer deformations that drag the scalp — no fine lip morphs). When the animation system sets an action unit, every part with that key deforms by its own authored delta, which is how hair/brows/eyelashes stay glued to the moving face. There are no emotion- or phoneme-named morphs — moods and visemes are both composed from these raw action units by the animation system.

    Named facial expressions (FXPD) — dialogue-driven bundles of action units

    The raw performance morphs above are the primitives; the engine composes named, higher-level expressions from them via the FXPD "Facial Expression" record — a flat, weighted bundle:

    FXPD: EDID + FULL(display name) + Morphs[] { MNAM = morph name, MWGT = weight 0..1 }

    Starfield.esm ships ~31 of these (facialExpression_Afraid, _Angry, _Happy, _Depressed, _Disgust, _Flirt, _Pain, _Rage, _Shocked, _Smug, _Yawn, …), each listing 2–42 of the same action-unit names as the performance morph.dat. For example facialExpression_Afraid = browLowererL 0.638, innerBrowRaiseR 0.853, eyeOpenL/R 1.0, jawOpen 0.155, lipCornerDepressL/R ≈0.3, nostrilDilator 0.621, neckFlexL/R, … So an expression is just "set these AUs to these weights." This is the moddable successor to Skyrim/FO4's engine-internal Mood<Emotion> expression morph — see Morphs & Shape Keys → How dialogue drives these morphs for the .tri ancestry and the 8-emotion enum it replaced.

    How they're triggered (and what is not a link). No plugin record references an FXPD by FormID — DIAL, INFO, and SCEN have no FXPD field, and a full dump of those groups contains no reference to a facialExpression_* FormID. What dialogue and scenes do reference is the "Anim (Face) Archetype" keyword — a KYWD named AnimFaceArchetype*, carried in a reusable Animation struct (INFO responses, scene phases, packages) via its FLMV "Anim Face Archetype" field. Those archetype keywords name-parallel the FXPD set (AnimFaceArchetypeAngry ↔ facialExpression_Angry, …Afraid ↔ …Afraid, …Shocked, …Depressed, …Disgust, …Smirk, …), but only by name: their FormIDs are unrelated (KYWD AnimFaceArchetypeAngry [000FA84A], FXPD facialExpression_Angry [0020D3A2]). So the trigger path is dialogue/scene → AnimFaceArchetype keyword → (in-house animation/behavior layer) → the matching FXPD morph bundle — the keyword↔FXPD binding is by name/convention in the anim system, not an editable plugin reference. (INFO responses additionally carry a per-line Emotion keyword + value in TRDA.)

    Tested in game (2026-10-08): the runtime reads FXPD, but only for the action units it already knows. Raising jawOpen to 1.0 in an override of facialExpression_Angry opened the jaw whenever Angry was applied in photo mode. Two morph names added to the same record (CanineEarBackL/R, defined with real deltas in the head's performance morph.dat) did nothing: not in photo mode, not in the CK's character-window expression preview, not on a re-baked NPC. Morphs are matched by name, not by their position in morph.dat; the head tested had its keys in a different order from vanilla and every vanilla action unit still worked. Tested through photo mode and the CK, not through a dialogue line.

    Furry-race implications. - Because an FXPD references morphs by name, and every face part only responds to the AU keys its own performance/morph.dat defines, a muzzle head that authors the vanilla AU names (browLowererL, jawOpen, innerBrowRaise, …) is driven by the vanilla dialogue expressions automatically — no behavior-graph authoring, no record edits. This is the cheapest path to dialogue-driven emotion on a non-human head. - FXPD records are global, not per-race. There is no race field and dialogue picks the expression, not the actor's race — so you cannot give an anthro wolf a different _Angry mix from a human by adding a race-specific expression alone. - New action units don't work. Adding species morphs (ear-back, ear-droop) to the head's performance/morph.dat and naming them in the global FXPD records does nothing: the engine ignores action-unit names outside its own set (tested above). - Fold species motion into vanilla action units instead (follows from the test; not itself tested yet). Add the ear-back deltas into the head's own browLowererL/R, which facialExpression_Angry weights at 0.84, and the ears move wherever those brows do: every expression and dialogue animation that uses them, with no record edits. The trade-off is that the ears follow that action unit everywhere, not just in one expression.

    The morph-delta file (.morph / morph.dat)

    Stored under Data/meshes/morphs/…/morph.dat — always named morph.dat. Only the folder path varies and is stored in the plugin file (e.g. meshes/morphs/human/female/chargen/body/morph.dat); not hash-named like .mesh files. Binary layout verified against Outfit Studio's SFMorphFile (little-endian):

    char   magic[4] = "MDAT"
uint32 numAxis            // always 3
uint32 numVertices
uint32 numShapeKeys
  per shape key:  uint32 nameLen + name bytes (no null terminator)
uint32 numMorphData
uint32 numOffsets         // == numVertices
morphData[numMorphData]   // 16 bytes each:
    uint16 offset[3]      //   position delta, half-float, in metric (.mesh) units;
                          //     × havokScale (69.969) → game units, as with .mesh positions
    uint16 targetVertColor//   target vertex colour, RGB565 (5:6:5). Default gray 0xBDF7 (~0.74)
    uint32 normal         //   delta normal,  DEC3N signed (axis = (n>>k & 1023)/511.5 − 1.0)
    uint32 tangent        //   delta tangent, DEC3N signed
offsets[numOffsets]       // 20 bytes each — sparse per-vertex:
    uint32 dataStart      //   start index into morphData
    uint32 keyMarker[4]   //   128-bit bitfield → which shape keys touch this vertex (max 128 keys)

    So a morph is dense over vertices (one offsets entry each, numOffsets == numVertices) but sparse over keys: a vertex stores a run of deltas only for the shape keys whose bit is set in its keyMarker, and the set bits (ascending) map 1:1 to that vertex's consecutive morphData records. Position deltas share the .mesh's havokScale factor.

    Corrections (verified byte-exact against vanilla + the SGB MorphIO.cpp source, 2026-07-14): the normal/tangent are DEC3N signed (earlier notes said UDEC3), and the position half-float is stored metric and multiplied — not divided — by havokScale to reach game units. A round-trip reader/writer reproduces a vanilla morph.dat byte-for-byte with this layout.

    targetVertColor is an RGB565 per-vertex colour, constant per vertex across all of that vertex's morph keys (verified: for chargen/coily_mohawk_f, 0 of 10,011 multi-key vertices differ across keys; same for the head). SF vertex colour drives material masks (hair/clothing use R/G/B channels — HairSettingsComponent.AOVertexColorChannel / DepthOffsetMaskVertexColorChannel; AlphaBlenderSettings.VertexColorChannel; chromatic values like magenta (1,0,1); faces use a near-gray baked AO 0xBDF7). It's a constant on most morphs — of 1194 vanilla morph.dat, only 49 carry a non-constant colour. It correlates with the mesh's own vertex colour but is not byte-equal (on the hair sample R/B match, G differs); the exact relationship is unresolved.

    Because it's one value per vertex (not per morph), it maps to a Blender colour attribute: PyNifly import writes the per-vertex colours there, export RGB565-encodes them back (gray default if absent) — so a morph round-trips from mesh + shape keys + one colour attribute, no per-morph colour authoring. (Whether the engine reads these colours at all is unconfirmed.) Note that the .mesh file does carry vertex colors and they are used.

    Starfield Geometry Bridge to Blender workflow (as of 09-2026): - Import the .mesh first, then import the .morph onto the active mesh. - Model needs a Basis shape key (base look) plus shape keys named to match the morph keys; all shape-key values range 0.0–1.0, relative to Basis. The engine does not allow "oversliding" past 1.0. - Custom chargen sliders are wired up with a JSON config whose "Key" must match the morph name in the .morph file, exposed in the creator via the CharGenMenu mod (there is no vanilla UI hook to add brand-new sliders).

    Reference implementations: Outfit Studio SFMorphFile.{h,cpp} (reads and writes) and StarfieldMeshConverter's MorphIO. Both are self-contained (~250 lines) with no NIF-library dependency beyond half-float + DEC3N helpers.

    PyNifly to Blender workflow: - Import the nif; it will bring the mesh with it even if the mesh is external. - Import the .morph on the active mesh separately; there's no link between nif and mesh. - Any morphs not part of the above "performance" set will be exported to the chagen morph file.

    Two ways to change face shape — bone morphs vs. vertex morphs

    Starfield has two independent systems that both reshape a face, with very different costs.

    Facebones morphs Vertex morphs
    Enabled in <RaceEDID>FacialBoneRegions<Sex>.txt (JSON) chargen/…/morph.dat
    Wired by file naming convention off the race EDID MRPH TCMP + race MPGN/MPGM
    Selected per NPC by the Face Morphs array (FMRI region + FMRG group + FMRS) and the Face Dial Positions array (FMSI + signed FMRS) — see below the Morph Groups array (BMPN "Blend Name" + BMPV "Intensity")
    Can express translate / rotate / scale of existing face bones arbitrary per-vertex displacement
    Propagates to attached parts yes, automatically — anything skinned to faceBone_* follows no — each part must re-author the same named keys

    The propagation difference is the big one. A vertex morph only deforms the mesh it was authored for, so every eyebrow, beard, hair, teeth and eyelash mesh needs its own morph.dat carrying the same key names — the identical tax the performance/expression AUs pay (see above). A bone morph moves the skeleton, so every part weighted to those bones follows for free, including headgear, as long as the bone weights match.

    The tradeoff is expressive range: bones can only move, rotate and scale what is already in the rig. Skull proportions, muzzle length, ear size and set are well within reach; fine surface detail that has no bone under it is not.

    What shipped custom races actually do (as of 09-2026): The published Felid race puts its per-species head shapes in the chargen morph.dat — its head chargen morph carries 29 keys including Tiger_Cheeks, Jaguar_Jaw, SnowLeopard_Nose, PointedEars_Ears, matching the race's MPGM morph-group members one-for-one, and NPCs select them via BMPN/BMPV. Its FacialBoneRegions files are a renamed copy of the vanilla human ones, phenotype names and all. The cost shows up exactly where the table predicts: its eyebrow chargen morph re-authors seven of the same keys (Tiger_Forehead, Jaguar_Forehead, SnowLeopard_Nose, …) so brows track the species head shape.

    Putting per-species shapes in the bone region file instead, as additional SculptRegion: false phenotype regions with matching race FMRU entries, gets that propagation for free — at the price of being limited to bone transforms. For a race whose sub-species differ mainly in skull proportion this is the cheaper architecture; for one that needs genuinely different surface topology it is not sufficient on its own. The two systems compose, so a hybrid (bones for proportion, a few vertex morphs for detail) is available.

    See the Creation Kit page, §8, for the FacialBoneRegions file schema, locations and the vanilla sculpt-region/slider inventory.

    How an NPC stores its face — three arrays, two systems

    An NPC_ record carries three separate face arrays. They look redundant — two of them hold what appears to be the same phenotype-and-region weighting — but they feed the two systems above, which have to be driven independently.

    NPC array Fields Drives
    Face Morphs FMRI region ID + FMRG group + FMRS weight (0…1) bones — apply one phenotype region's slider, restricted to one face group
    Face Dial Positions FMSI slider ID + FMRS position (signed, −1…+1) bones — one individual sculpt slider
    Morph Groups BMPN key + BMPV weight vertices — one key in the chargen morph.dat

    Face Morphs → bone morphs. FMRI is the ID of a SculptRegion: false region in FacialBoneRegions<Sex>.txt. That region holds a single ZeroToOne slider driving all 48 face bones at once — a whole base face. FMRG then names one of the nine columns of the …Mapping.csv (Cheeks, Chin, Ears, Eyes, Forehead, Jaw, Mouth, Neck, Nose), which is a per-bone mask: the CSV says how much each bone belongs to that group, 0–100. So one entry reads "apply phenotype male_as_md1 at 0.24, but only to the bones the Cheeks column selects." That is how a single 48-bone slider becomes nine independently blendable regions.

    Face Dial Positions → the sculpt sliders. FMSI indexes a slider inside a SculptRegion: true region, not the region itself — the Mouth region (ID 22) owns sliders 24, 25, 76, 77 — which is why dial indices fall in the gaps between region IDs. Values are signed because these sliders are bipolar (ZeroToOne: false): 'down - up', 'narrow - wide', 'underbite - overbite'.

    Morph Groups → vertex morphs. BMPN is a literal key in the chargen morph.dat. For a phenotype-grid morph the key is the composed <phenotype>_<region> name, which is exactly what Face Morphs expresses as a pair — hence the apparent duplication. But the array is not limited to the grid: it is also where body-build keys (Overweight, Thin, Strong) and a custom race's species morphs live, since those exist only as vertex data.

    What the vanilla data shows

    Measured over all 7,131 NPC_ records in Starfield.esm, resolving FMRI through HumanRace's region table and composing <phenotype>_<FMRG>:
    carry both Face Morphs and Morph Groups 2,982
    carry Morph Groups only 140
    carry Face Morphs only 0
    composed key in both, identical value 38,241
    composed key in both, differing value 25,501
    key only in Face Morphs (bone-only shaping) 6,595
    key only in Morph Groups (no bone equivalent) 10,931

    Two things follow. The 38,241 exact value matches confirm the composition rule BMPN == <phenotype>_<FMRG> — the two arrays really are addressing the same conceptual blend. But they are not copies of each other: the values diverge on 25,501 entries in both directions, and each array carries thousands of keys the other lacks. The creator writes both from one slider, then the two systems are tuned separately.

    Face Morphs never appears without Morph Groups, in any vanilla NPC. Vertex morphs stand alone; bone morphs are always accompanied. A custom race can therefore ship vertex morphs with no rig work at all — which is what the published felid race does — but should not expect the reverse to be a well-trodden path.

    4. Tints, complexions & overlays — the AVM system (AVMD/AVMS)

    Starfield replaced the fixed [Skyrim] tint-mask layers with a generalized Additive Visual Material (AVM) system. Individual races have no tint layer list -- there's one for all races.

    4.1 Per-NPC tint application (NPC_ → "AVMD Tints")

    Tints are applied on the NPC_ record — Each NPC carries an EDCT Tint Count plus an AVMD Tints array; each entry is one applied layer:

    Field Meaning
    MNAM the group type — Simple Group / Complex Group / Modulation.
    TNAM Tint Group the slot — one of the layer names in §4.3 (Dermaesthetic, Accents1, Lipstick2…). Combines with MNAM to give the EDID of the target group.
    QNAM Tint Name which option within the group
    VNAM Tint Texture the texture file path
    NNAM Tint Color RGBA color
    INTV Intensity 1–128

    The entry is self-contained. It carries the texture path, the color and the intensity, so nothing at runtime has to resolve the group in order to find the asset. Composited over the face material at runtime or during facegen. The NPC also names its per-slot colors directly as strings (HCOL hair, FHCL facial hair, BCOL eyebrow, ECOL eye, JCOL jewelry).

    The FaceGen bake uses the stored VNAM literally — it never re-resolves the option through its group.

    • Measured: 577 of 20,751 vanilla NPC tint entries (in ComplexionMask1/2, Dermaesthetic and TattooMask) store a path that exists in no archive, because the texture was renamed or moved after the NPC was authored (moles_many_small_02.dds → …_mask.dds, …_derm.dds → …_derm_color.dds, faces\makeup\tattoo_makeup_geometric_mask.dds → faces\chargen\postblenddetails\tattootint\…). Their QNAM option resolves through the group to a real file but it is not used. TattooMask is the control, since a tattoo is unmistakable in a baked _color: of the 7 vanilla NPCs carrying one, 5 store a valid path and 2 a stale one. The valid tattoos checked (skull, eagle, skull, leaf) are in the baked texture; both stale ones — Valerie Mosquera and the City_AC_FABQ10_Visitor — are absent. A stale VNAM silently drops the layer, and Bethesda ships hundreds of them.
    • Also: NNAM (0,0,0,0) is not invisible — those skulls bake black.

    For an author this means renaming or moving a tint texture breaks every NPC already authored against it, and fixing the group does not repair them; the records have to be re-saved.

    Colour or opacity — the slots split cleanly. Measured over every vanilla NPC carrying tints: Dermaesthetic (2,949 NPCs) and Complexion1/Complexion2 (2,432 / 1,279) always carry NNAM = (0,0,0,0) and vary only by INTV, so they are pure texture overlays differing in opacity alone. ComplexionMask1/ComplexionMask2 (2,661 / 2,290) are the layers that actually shift hue, carrying 34 distinct RGBA values backed by their paired 29-entry Modulation group. Across every tint slot in Starfield.esm, only 142 distinct RGBA values appear — authored from a working palette, even though the field is free-form.

    One layer per slot. Across 3,800 NPCs in Starfield.esm, ShatteredSpace.esm and the SFBGS plugins, no NPC ever lists the same TNAM twice — and neither does the shipped Felid race across its 92 tinted NPCs. A slot is single-occupancy. The most any vanilla NPC carries is 16 layers; the median is 7–8.

    Two consequences for anyone authoring a race. To show several marks at once you either bake them into one option — Burn_AlloverLight and Burn_LeftCheekManySmall are pre-composited multi-scar textures — or spend one slot per mark. The number of concurrent overlays is capped by the slot list, not by anything per-NPC.

    4.2 The AVMD record

    AVMD ("AVMS Data") records define the tint/complexion groups and options themselves. Starfield.esm holds 6,154 of them — 2,399 Simple, 2,029 Complex, 1,726 Modulation.

    Field Meaning
    EDID e.g. SimpleGroup_Dermaesthetic, Modulation_ComplexionMask1
    MNAM group type: None / Simple Group / Complex Group / Modulation
    YNAM the group's name
    TNAM the group's identifier — this is the lookup key used in other records
    ITMC entry count
    entries LNAM option name, plus VNAM (texture or typed reference) and/or NNAM (color)
    MODT texture-type flags — Rough / Opacity / Normal / AO / Metal / Biome Conditions / Emissive. Not always present; when present not always correct. Left blank for aldebo.
    AVMP optional parent AVMD

    Everything in this system resolves by NAME, never by FormID. Nothing holds an AVMD's FormID — not the RACE, not the NPC, not another AVMD. Records are found by combining the group type with the TNAM name ID, and possibly other elements. So SimpleGroup_ColorlessAccents1_Color, SimpleGroup_ColorlessAccents1_ao, SimpleGroup_Chargen_ColorlessAccents1 are all found by building the editor ID from the parts. Similarly Modulation_Lipstick1 is associated with SimpleGroup_Lipstick1 only by editor ID.

    Three AVM group kinds:

    Kind Entry = Used for
    Simple Group (type 1) LNAM name + VNAM texture path a texture option (skin tone, eye/teeth/hair texture, a face overlay)
    Complex Group (type 2) LNAM name + reference to another AVMD bundles channels, or exposes a group for a head part's color mapping
    Modulation (type 3) LNAM name + NNAM color a solid color option for another group such as lipstick, tattoo ink, or jewelry

    A Complex Group entry references another AVMD two ways, and the bare form dominates about 50:1:

    • bare LNAM (5,728 uses) — the name alone. Presumably tried against the Simple, Complex, and Modulation groups until a hit is found.
    • VNAM = <Prefix>_<Name> (114 uses) — SimpleGroup_ / ComplexGroup_ / Modulation_ reference includes the group type explicitly. Used even when more than one group is found and will be used. (E.g. ComplexGroup_PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers references Modulation_ComplexionMask1 but will access SimpleGroup_ComplexionMask1 as well.)

    ⚠️ The two forms are not interchangeable in a given record — where a Complex Group uses VNAM, the LNAM beside it is only a label. ComplexGroup_FaceSkinTones has LNAM 'male_af_md1' with VNAM 'SimpleGroup_FaceSkinTones_male_af_md1', and a bare male_af_md1 matches no record at all.

    Modulation groups are reached almost exclusively from Complex Groups — 1,684 of the 1,726 are referenced that way (mostly the creature/biome color sets). No NPC ever names a Modulation group directly: of all vanilla NPC tint entries, 20,751 name a Simple Group, 4,298 a Complex Group, and zero a Modulation. The remaining 34 are reached by name-pairing (§4.4).

    Face overlays (complexion, dermaesthetic, scars, tattoos, lipstick, eyeshadow, cheeks, accents…) are Simple Groups whose textures live under textures/actors/human/faces/chargen/postblenddetails/<category>/ and apply only to the "Face" head part.

    The groups a custom race usually has to touch

    These groups are global — nothing scopes an AVMD to a race. There is no race field naming them (a RACE names only BSTT/HSTT/FSTT and FCTP), and lookups go through the one game-wide name index. A race reaches them only through its head parts, one part at a time, so a custom race mixes vanilla and its own freely.

    Beyond the face overlays, six vanilla groups cover hair, eyes, brows and teeth. All six verified against Starfield.esm:

    Group FormID Kind Options Holds
    HairColor 000ECD2E Complex 8 the hair colour palettes
    FacialHairColor 000ECD30 Complex 1 beard colour
    EyebrowColor 000ECD2D Complex 12 brow colour
    EyebrowOpacity 0000EF9F Simple 16 brow opacity — a part with no entry renders transparent
    EyeColor 000377DC Simple 12 iris textures
    TeethCustomization 0006160A Simple 4 teeth textures

    How they're reached differs by feature, and only hair and brows involve the head part at all. Eye and teeth parts carry no NAM2/NAM3 — Human_Female_LeftEye has neither — because those groups are flat lists the NPC indexes by name directly:

    Feature Group Shape Branch chosen by Option chosen by
    eyes EyeColor Simple, 12 options (Hazel, Iron, …) Hardwired lookup in SimpleGroup_EyeColor NPC ECOL
    teeth TeethCustomization Simple, 4 options (Teeth_Clean, …) Hardwired lookup in SimpleGroup_TeethCustomization NPC TETC
    hair HairColor Complex, 8 entries — one per hair type Simple group identified by head part's NAM2 (e.g. Hair_Short) NPC HCOL
    beards FacialHairColor Complex, 1 entry head part NAM2 (FacialHair_Shared) NPC FHCL
    brows EyebrowColor Complex, 12 entries — one per brow type head part NAM2 (Eyebrows_StandardMale) NPC BCOL

    So the Complex colour groups are indexed by the part's NAM2 value: the part says which kind of hair or brow it is, that picks the branch, and the NPC's colour string picks an option inside it. NAM3 names the colour group itself on the 25 brow parts (EyebrowColor), and on 109 hair parts it instead names a scalp-mask option (FadedAfro_Cap_Mask) inside HairFollicleMask — the field does double duty. Vanilla writes the literal ' NONE' (with a leading space) where a part has no value. As everywhere in this system, all of these are name lookups — nothing holds a FormID.

    The shipped Felid race shows the mixing in practice. Of its 22 head parts carrying these fields, NAM2 names a vanilla branch 17 times (Hair_Short, Hair_Afro, Eyebrows_Apart, FacialHair_Shared) and its own Eyebrows_Hair_Short the other 5 — which is why it also ships an AVMP child extending EyebrowColor, since a new brow type needs a branch in the colour group to match. Every brow part names vanilla EyebrowColor in NAM3; its hair and beards carry ' NONE'.

    So a race needs its own group only where its artwork demands one, and AVMP (see above) is how a new branch or option joins a vanilla group instead of replacing it.

    Skin tones work differently again: the race names its groups in BSTT/HSTT/FSTT and indexes them positionally with STON, so the option names there carry no meaning. That whole mechanism is §4.5.

    Adding options in bulk. Either edit the groups directly in xEdit, or generate them with the config-driven RTFP patcher ([AVMData] / [FormList] add_entr directives, minver=115), which is how the CharGen Resources resource ships its additions.

    AVMP — add options to a vanilla group without overriding it

    AVMP ("AVMS Parent", per SF1Edit: wbFormIDCk(AVMP, 'AVMS Parent', [AVMD])) is the one place this system uses a FormID. A child AVMD names a parent AVMD, and the child's options are added to the parent's set — the parent record is never touched, so two mods can extend the same slot without conflicting. It is the conflict-free alternative to overriding a vanilla group.

    Measured across the official plugins and the shipped Felid race:

    • The base game never uses it: 0 of 6,154 AVMDs. Only ShatteredSpace.esm (3) and Felid (5). (Raw AVMP byte matches inside Starfield.esm are false positives in compressed record data — count subrecords, not bytes.)
    • Always exactly one AVMP per record, and always the last subrecord.
    • Bethesda's own naming convention is <ParentTNAM>_AVMDEXT; Felid copies it.
    • In all 8 real cases the child and parent share zero option names — children add, never replace — and are always the same kind (Simple extends Simple, Complex extends Complex).
    • Extend both the chargen-side and the runtime-side group. ShatteredSpace extends Chargen_TattooMask and TattooMask; Felid extends Chargen_Lipstick2 and Lipstick2.
    child parent adds
    Chargen_TattooMask_AVMDEXT (ShatteredSpace) Chargen_TattooMask [00097579] 13 DLC tattoo masks
    HairFollicleMask_AVMDEXT (ShatteredSpace) HairFollicleMask [000A2711] 3 scalp masks
    SimpleGroup_Lipstick2_Felid_AVMDEXT Lipstick2 [00393D6F] 12 face patterns
    ComplexGroup_EyebrowColor_Felid_AVMDEXT EyebrowColor [000ECD2D] 1 eyebrow group

    This is the mechanism behind §4.9's "new records under new names": a distinct TNAM keeps the child out of the vanilla name index, and AVMP is what still delivers its options into the vanilla slot.

    ⚠️ AVMP can only add. A race that wants its own options instead of vanilla's has to change the group the creator reads from — see §4.11.

    4.3 The layer stack — ComplexGroup_PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers

    The set of slots lives in one record: ComplexGroup_PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers (AVMD:00393D76). Its 26 entries, in record order, describe the compositing stack bottom to top — but editing them does not change it, which is the subject of the experiment below:

     1 Dermaesthetic          10 ColorlessAccents2      19 Lipstick1
 2 ComplexionMask1        11 TattooColorMap *       20 Lipstick2
 3 Complexion1            12 TattooTint *           21 Eyeshadow1
 4 Complexion2            13 TattooMask             22 Eyeshadow2
 5 ComplexionMask2        14 MakeupFullPaintMask *  23 Eyeliner1
 6 Scars                  15 MakeupFullPaintMask1   24 Eyeliner2
 7 Accents1               16 MakeupFullPaintMask2   25 EnvironmentColor *
 8 Accents2               17 Cheeks1                26 EnvironmentMask
 9 ColorlessAccents1      18 Cheeks2                (* = broken, see below)

    Read as paint layers: skin detail → complexion → scars → accents → tattoos → makeup → blush → lips → eyeshadow → eyeliner → environmental grime last.

    An NPC's tint array is this list filtered to the layers in use. Verified over 3,020 NPCs carrying tints: zero pairs ever appear in conflicting order, and a topological sort of the observed orderings reproduces the record's order exactly, including the six pairs that never co-occur in any NPC.

    How the record is found: by hard-coded name. It has no AVMP parent, no record anywhere holds its FormID, and no RACE mentions it. The string PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers appears once in each of Starfield.exe and CreationKit.exe, in a cluster with other well-known group names (HairTypes) and the blend-mode literals. So the engine carries a short list of group names it looks up directly, and this is one of them.

    ⚠️ Skin layer order is built in

    The record documents the order; it does not define it. Neither reordering this record nor reordering an NPC's own tint array changes what composites on top.

    The experiment, on CF_NaevaMora [00009138], who ships with both a scar and an accent. Two vanilla textures were replaced with full-face opaque colours — scar_nosegouges_color.dds → red, accents_undereyebagssmall_color.dds → blue — and both layers set to the same intensity, so whichever composites last wins the whole face. The result was read out of the baked facecustomization\Starfield.esm\00009138_Color.dds, not judged by eye:

    Test Change Baked face
    1 vanilla order; scar only red (control — the layer works)
    2 vanilla order; scar + accent blue — Accents1 is above Scars
    3 NPC's stored tint array reversed (Accents1 before Scars) blue, unchanged
    4 this record reordered — Accents1 moved below Scars blue, unchanged

    Test 4's bake measured 99.8% pure blue, 0.1% red. The Creation Kit was restarted between the edit and the bake, so this is not the CK serving a cached load order. Test 3 also showed the CK's post-blend list displaying its own canonical order rather than the array's stored order — the same fixed sequence showing through in the editor UI.

    Two useful corollaries: a mod cannot re-layer the face, and the order vanilla data appears in is reliable documentation of the built-in sequence, since the built-in order and the record agree everywhere the data can show it (§4.3's topological check over 3,020 NPCs).

    For a custom race this is a hard constraint, not an inconvenience: a species marking that must sit under a scar has only the three slots below it available in the game UI — Dermaesthetic, Complexion1, Complexion2 — and everything else will layer over scars like makeup, whatever the marking actually represents.

    Not tested: the live player composite (the bake is the Creation Kit's), and whether deleting a slot from this record disables it — a removal would test the claim more sharply than a reorder does.

    ⚠️ The stack is global, not per-race. There is no race-level field pointing at a layer list — contrast the skin tones, which the race does nominate via BSTT/HSTT/FSTT. Every race draws on this one record, and a custom race gets no variant of its own. No vanilla DLC overrides it, and neither does the shipped Felid race — which, given the test above, would have gained them nothing.

    Four of the 26 entries are broken, and the game ships that way:

    Slot VNAM target Status
    TattooColorMap SimpleGroup_TattooColorMap no record of that name, under any type
    TattooTint SimpleGroup_TattooTint no record of that name
    MakeupFullPaintMask SimpleGroup_MakeupFullPaintMask no record — though …Mask1/…Mask2 both exist
    EnvironmentColor SimpleGroup_EnvironmentColor resolves, but has zero entries

    Checked against every AVMD in the base game plus ShatteredSpace and the SFBGS plugins. These are the same four slots no NPC ever uses, and TattooColorMap / TattooTint are also the only slot names absent from both executables — the signature of a cut feature whose code went away while the data entry stayed. The practical lesson: an unresolvable entry is skipped, not fatal.

    4.4 Slot name-pairing

    The VNAM in the layer record is not the only route to a slot's content. 15 of the 26 slots have a same-named AVMD of another type that the stack never references directly, found by the name alone:

    Stack names Slot also exists as Slots
    Modulation Simple ComplexionMask1/2, TattooMask, MakeupFullPaintMask1/2
    Simple Modulation Cheeks1/2, Lipstick1/2, Eyeshadow1/2, Eyeliner1/2, EnvironmentMask

    Which half the stack names looks like authoring accident — both resolve, and the engine finds the other by name regardless. The division of labour is consistent: the Simple group supplies the mask textures, the same-named Modulation group supplies the legal colors.

    Tattoos are the clearest worked example. The stack names Modulation_TattooMask (9 colors — Black, Brown, Red, Pink, Blue, Teal), while the designs live in SimpleGroup_TattooMask (13 — Dagger, Leaf, Cat, Circuits01, Dragon), which nothing references. All 7 NPCs using the slot address it as Simple.

    Evidence that the pairing is real: of 14,091 NPC tints in a slot that has a same-named Modulation group, 75.5% carry a color that appears in that group's palette (misses are dominated by "no color set", 00000000).

    ⚠️ A paired Modulation group cannot be pointed at — only be. Extending a slot's color options therefore requires overriding the vanilla Modulation record; extending its textures does not. This is exactly what the Felid race does (§4.7).

    What feeds each slot

    The 26 slots have 5 different semantics. Counts are vanilla option counts, measured per group kind:

    Fed by Slots Shape
    Full-colour texture Dermaesthetic (84), Complexion1 (13), Complexion2 (13) a Simple group of *_color.dds; the texture's own alpha is the strength
    Mask + palette ComplexionMask1/2 (46 masks, 29 colors), TattooMask (13, 9), MakeupFullPaintMask1/2 (21, 22), Cheeks1/2 (3, 40), Lipstick1/2 (5-6, 40), Eyeshadow1/2 (6, 40), Eyeliner1/2 (9, 40), EnvironmentMask (7, 5) Simple group of greyscale *_mask.dds + same-named Modulation palette (§4.4)
    Four coordinated channels Scars, Accents1/2 a Complex group of 4 entries fanning out to _color / _normal / _rough / _ao Simple groups
    Four coordinated channels, monochrome ColorlessAccents1/2 same as above but only the alpha channel of the "color" texture is used.
    Nothing TattooColorMap, TattooTint, MakeupFullPaintMask, EnvironmentColor the four broken slots (§4.3)

    Only the first three slots in the stack — Dermaesthetic, Complexion1, Complexion2 — paint below Scars. Everything above is conceived as makeup, paint or grime that layes over the natural skin. That is the constraint for a furry race: fur markings that should sit under a scar have just those three slots, one of which (Complexion1/2) ships as colour-temperature washes. Markings placed in Cheeks or Lipstick will layer over scars as though they were blush.

    The game UI labels differ from the record names: Complexion1 appears as "Complexion Color Temp" and ComplexionMask2 as "Complexion Blemishes".

    4.5 Skin tones and STON

    STON (NPC "Skin Tone Index") is a positional index into the entry list of the skin-tone Simple Group the race nominates. One value resolves three times:

    NPC.STON = 3
  RACE.BSTT "Male_Naked_Body_Skin_Tones" (Simple)  -> entry[3] = NakedBodyM_sk3_color
  RACE.HSTT "Male_Hands"                 (Simple)  -> entry[3] = DefaultHandsM_sk3_color
  RACE.FSTT "FaceSkinTones"              (Complex) -> per-phenotype Simple
                                                   -> entry[3] = male_af_md1_sk3_color

    One index keeps body, hands and face on the same tone — which is what makes the neck seam match. The face path is two levels: FSTT names a Complex Group whose 22 entries are the phenotype names, resolving (via prefixed VNAMs) to SimpleGroup_FaceSkinTones_<phenotype>.

    Seen whole, a race's skin tones are a grid: one column per list, one row per tone.

                     BSTT                 HSTT                 FSTT  (Complex Group)
                 Body Skin Tone       Hand Skin Tone       Face Skin Tone
                     |                    |                    | 
                     |                    |                    v
Complex Group        |                    |         per phenotype (sex_ethnicity_age)
                     |                    |            +---------+---------+
                     v                    v            v         v         v
Simple Group       Body                 Hands       female_af_md1  …  male_eu_yo1
             +------------------+------------------+-------------+---+-------------+
 STON 0      | SkinTone1        | SkinTone1        | SkinTone1   | … | SkinTone1   |
 STON 1      | SkinTone2        | SkinTone2        | SkinTone2   | … | SkinTone2   |
 STON 2      | SkinTone3        | SkinTone3        | SkinTone3   | … | SkinTone3   |
   …         | …                | …                | …           | … | …           |
 STON N      | SkinToneN        | SkinToneN        | SkinToneN   | … | SkinToneN   |
             +------------------+------------------+-------------+---+-------------+

    STON picks a row, and the row has to exist in every column — the body list is the master count, and hands, every phenotype's face list, and the dermaesthetic filter (§4.11) must all match it. That is the rule the CK validators below state four different ways.

    The cost of one extra tone is therefore one texture per column, not one texture. A race with P phenotypes pays P + 2 albedo textures per tone — one body, one hands, and one per phenotype face. Vanilla human runs 22 phenotypes × 9 tones = 198 face albedos (plus 9 body and 9 hands), and every phenotype supplies a texture for every tone whether it suits that phenotype or not.

    ⚠️ STON is stored 0-based and displayed 1-based. The xEdit definition is wbEnum(['1'..'9']) over a uint8, so raw 0 shows as "1". The raw byte is the index and lines up with the sk0…sk8 entry names.

    Group length is not fixed at nine. Humans use 9, Child_* skin-tone groups have 3, Mannequin_Body_and_Hands has 1, and the Felid race ships 19. Nine is the human convention and the enum's ceiling, not a requirement — but STON must stay inside whatever length the group has. There is headroom: the largest Simple group in shipped data is 84 entries (Dermaesthetic), and Felid ships 19 tones. ⚠ Do not assume the uint8 means 256 is reachable, though — the engine carries a named maximum, SkinToneCountMaxC ("is out of range SkinToneCountMaxC [0..%u)"), whose value is not known. Verified over every NPC_ in Starfield.esm, the range tracks the group length exactly:

    race entries in its groups STON values used NPCs
    HumanRace 9 0–8 (all nine) 3,326
    ChildRace 3 0–2 51
    MannequinRace 1 0 75
    no skin-tone group (robots, fauna) — 0 —

    ⚠ The index is positional, not parsed from the filename. HumanRace is tidy — entry N is …_skN_… in body, hands and face alike — which makes STON look like it means "the skN texture". It does not. ChildRace runs entry 0 = female_child_01_body_color (no suffix at all), 1 = …_sk2_…, 2 = …_sk3_…. The only real requirement is that the body, hands and face lists are the same length and in the same order; if they drift, an NPC gets a face that does not match its body, silently.

    The Creation Kit enforces this, in its own words. Its validator strings state the rule outright, and name the body skin-tone group as the master count everything else must match:

    • "Validates that the number of subtype entries for hand skin tones matches the number of entries for body skin tones."
    • "Validates that the number of subtype entries for each type of face skin tone matches the number of entries for body skin tones."
    • "Validates that the number of subtype entries for the complex group DermaestheticChargen (dermaesthetic skin tone filters) matches the number of entries for body skin tones."
    • "Validates that all face skin tone labels are prefixed with 'male' or 'female', and there is a subtype label for the default skin tone textures of each sex."

    The individual error messages show what each rule actually checks — and that the default is checked separately from the phenotypes:

    • "Empty skin tone subtype at facial bone region '%d' on %s."
    • "Invalid skin tone subtype '%s' at facial bone region '%d' on %s. (should be a valid subtype from '%s')" — a phenotype row names a face the group has no label for.
    • "Facial skin tone type '%s' at facial bone region '%d' on %s contains %d subtypes but should have %d subtypes."
    • "Invalid default %s skin tone subtype '%s' on %s. (should be a valid subtype from '%s')"
    • "default %s skin tone subtype '%s' on %s contains %d subtypes but should have %d subtypes."

    ⚠ Every face skin-tone group needs a male_default and a female_default label, whether or not the race has any phenotypes. The default is not a phenotype — it has no FMRI row (see below) — so it is easy to leave out; the CK checks it on its own, both that it exists and that its group is as long as the body's. male_default and female_default are also literal strings in Starfield.exe, so the game looks them up by name as well. Every vanilla race that has face skin tones carries both:

    race FSTT group labels FMRI phenotype rows per sex defaults
    HumanRace, its SFBGS…_HumanRace copies, SFBGS001_RedeemedRace FaceSkinTones 22 9 both
    ChildRace FaceSkinTones_Child 2 0 both

    ChildRace is the minimal shape: no phenotype table at all, and a face group that is nothing but the two defaults, each pointing at a Simple group as long as the child body group. A race that needs one face per sex can copy it.

    It confirms the 0-based storage too — "The skinToneIndex should be in range [0..%u] (which is range [1..%u] in the CK gui) as defined in AVMS simple group" — and that an out-of-range STON is "clamp[ed] to range at runtime", not fatal. Note the count is read from the AVMS Simple group, which is also what the in-game creator uses to size its skin-tone slider.

    ⚠ Only the face has a phenotype axis. BSTT and HSTT name a Simple Group directly, so body and hand colour is exactly that group's length — nine variants, shared by every human in the game. FSTT goes through the Complex Group, so the face gets 22 × 9 = 198 albedos. The body and hands also have no filename route: %s\%s_sk%u_color.dds is the only skin-tone-indexed texture format string in either executable, and it is the face one, so BSTT/HSTT → AVMD is the sole way a skin tone ever reaches a body. A race cannot skip the AVMD groups by following the filename convention. A race whose sub-types differ by more than lightness — fur pattern, species markings — cannot express that on the body through this mechanism at all, because the body path never sees the phenotype. Lengthening the body ladder does not help either, since the phenotype and STON are selected independently (below).

    On the body and hands, a skin tone changes only the albedo. Every entry in a BSTT/HSTT group is a _color texture, and nothing in the chain names any other map. Normal, roughness and AO are fixed paths in the .mat — slots 1, 3 and 5 of the base texture set — shared by every tone. Vanilla, verified in Naked_M_Body.mat, Naked_F_Body.mat and DefaultHandsM.mat:

    slot Naked_M_Body.mat base texture set
    0 nakedbodym_sk3_color.dds — the albedo the skin tone replaces
    1 nakedbodym_normal.dds
    3 nakedbodym_rough.dds
    5 NakedBodyM_ao.dds

    So one body material cannot give different tones different normals or roughness; a per-species fur normal needs a different material, which means a different body.

    A body (hands, face) material is made up of multiple layers and the second and higher levels are composited with a mask. The _mask texture is not a skin-tone mask. These materials (BodySkin2Layer shader) layers carrying only a detail normal — faces\FaceDetails\Young_Cheek_detail_normal.dds, tiled 50 × 50 on the body and 15 × 15 on the hands — and NakedBodyM_mask.dds / defaulthandsm_mask.dds is slot 0 of Blender1, the blender that lays that detail over the base layer. It controls where the micro-detail goes; the skin-tone albedo is not masked at all.

    How the phenotype maps are chosen and blended

    FSTT names an AVMD Complex Group — ComplexGroup_FaceSkinTones for humans. That record is a lookup table: each entry pairs an LNAM, a phenotype name such as male_as_md1, with a VNAM naming the Simple Group it leads to (SimpleGroup_FaceSkinTones_male_as_md1).

    The phenotype name then reaches the face albedo by two separate routes, both keyed on that same name:

    route how the albedo is found
    AVMD the Complex Group entry whose LNAM matches → its Simple Group → entry [STON] → that entry's VNAM texture path
    filename built from a format string compiled into Starfield.exe and CreationKit.exe

    The format string is %s\%s_sk%u_color.dds, in C printf notation: %s inserts a string and %u an unsigned integer. Filled in, the first %s is the race's FCTP directory, the second is the phenotype name, and %u is the NPC's STON — so actors\FSFCanine\faces\chargen\male_as_md1_sk3_color.dds. Where the AVMD route resolves it wins; the normal, roughness and AO maps have only the filename route (Creation Kit §3).

    Either way, a route resolves one map for one phenotype. A face is not made of one, though: the single %s is one call, not one phenotype. The engine resolves a map per source and composites them region by region, and both executables carry the machinery to do it, sitting in the string table beside the format string above:

    string in Starfield.exe and CreationKit.exe what it is
    %s\FCT_%s_mask.dds the mask for one face region — %s is the FCTP directory, then a region name
    %s\FCT_null_mask.dds a hard-coded maskless variant; when it is used is unconfirmed
    uMaxBlendSources:FCT ini setting capping how many sources one face may blend
    fBlendIntensityThresholdPerRegion:FCT the per-region weight below which a source is dropped
    bBlendValidation:FCT validation toggle

    The masks exist and they line up exactly: Actors\Human\Faces\Chargen ships FCT_Cheeks_mask.dds, _Chin_, _Ears_, _Eyes_, _Forehead_, _Jaw_, _Mouth_, _Neck_ and _Nose_ — the nine MPGN/FMRG region names, one for one — plus FCT_null_mask.dds.

    None of the three settings appears in any shipped .ini, so their defaults are compiled in and unknown.

    This applies to _normal, _rough and _ao as much as to the albedo: they are keyed on the phenotype by the same kind of format string, so they are blended from the same sources. A custom race needs the full set for every phenotype it can blend, not just for its default.

    Nothing on the NPC stores those names. Checked across all 7,131 NPC_ records in Starfield.esm: not one carries a FaceSkinTones key verbatim in any subrecord. They are reached indirectly, which is why they are easy to miss:

    NPC Face Morphs:   FMRI (row id) + FMRG (face group) + FMRS (weight 0...1)
RACE FMRI/FMRU:    row id -> "male_as_md1"      (looked up in the NPC's OWN sex's table)
FSTT Complex:      LNAM "male_as_md1" -> SimpleGroup_FaceSkinTones_male_as_md1
STON:                                 -> entry[N]

    So the keys are a property of the face-morph blend, not a stored field.

    ⚠ The FMRI id space is per sex. HumanRace uses id 1 for male_as_md1 and for female_eu_md2, and id 9 for male_af_md1 and female_af_md1. Resolve an NPC's FMRI through the table of its own sex — ACBS flag bit 0 is Female — never through a single merged lookup. Merging them silently relabels those male phenotypes as female ones, and every statistic built on top is quietly wrong.

    That blend is rarely a single phenotype:

    phenotypes blended NPCs
    1 420
    2–4 748
    5–6 1,187
    7–9 636

    Median 5–6. And the mixing happens inside a region, not only between regions — counting the distinct phenotypes that contribute to the same FMRG region, across the same NPCs:

    phenotypes in one region region instances
    1 5,082
    2 1,533
    3 592
    4–5 240

    2,954 of 8,036 region instances — 37% — draw on more than one phenotype, up to eight (FC_Neon_GenericCustomerM01's Neck alone blends six), so uMaxBlendSources is at least 8. The weights are not normalised either: per region they sum to anywhere from 0.0 to 2.0, median 0.49.

    <sex>_default is never authored. Zero of the 2,991 NPCs with a resolvable blend include it — it is purely the fallback for an actor whose blend resolves to nothing, which is why an unbaked or preset actor bakes out as male_default_sk0_color.dds.

    Four of the 22 keys are unreachable by blending. HumanRace has 9 FMRI rows per sex, and the diff is clean in one direction: every blendable phenotype has a texture group, but male_default, female_default, male_eu_md1 and female_eu_md1 have no FMRI row. The two defaults are the hard-coded fallback, so the genuinely dead keys are just the two eu_md1 entries. Instead eu_md2 entries exist and are used, the only ethnicity/age combination to use an index other than 1. A Complex Group entry with no matching FMRI row in the race is dead weight — easy to add to a custom race and never reach. (The reverse — a phenotype row with no entry — is worse, and the Creation Kit rejects it: see the validator rules above.)

    Phenotype and STON are independent axes. Correlated in vanilla, but not linked: every one of the 18 phenotypes (9 per sex) appears with 4–9 distinct STON values, and female_af_md1 and male_af_md1 each span all nine (321 and 88 HumanRace NPCs). Mean STON is 3.38 for af phenotypes, 1.57 for eu and 1.09 for as; af and as span the full 0–8 range and eu 0–7. Authors pick sensible pairings; the format enforces none, so a creator can put any face on any body tone.

    4.6 Dermaesthetic is not tied to the skin tone

    A natural assumption, and wrong. The Dermaesthetic group's options are named for a phenotype and a tone (Asian_Male_Ol1_Sk1 → male_as_ol1_sk1_derm_color.dds), which looks like it should track STON. It does not, and it cannot: the group only ships four tone bands — sk1, sk3, sk6, sk8 — uniformly across all 21 phenotypes it covers, so five of the nine STON values have no matching texture at all.

    Measured over 2,949 NPCs carrying both: 466 agree (15.8%), 2,483 do not. Mean dermaesthetic band does rise with STON (2.79 at STON 0 → 6.51 at 5) but every STON value draws on all four bands. The phenotype matches one the NPC actually blends only 45.7% of the time, and the sex disagrees on 739 NPCs — the retail CF06_LvlSyndicate_Unaggressive_F01 is female, blends three female phenotypes, and wears male_as_md2_sk6_derm_color.dds.

    The layer is an independent overlay carrying subtle skin detail, picked by eye. It does not need to be authored as a phenotype × tone matrix. Note that the alpha on vanilla textures goes to 0 at the neck seam, so they'll blend with the body.

    Nor is it required. It is the most-used slot (3,633 entries across the official plugins), but of the 2,094 HumanRace NPCs with their own appearance data (not inherited from a template), 323 have no Dermaesthetic layer:

    NPCs
    Dermaesthetic present 1,771
    other tint layers, but no Dermaesthetic 46
    no tint layers at all 277

    The 46 are fully tinted faces with the slot simply unused — named characters among them (Gerhard Pohl, Captain Petrov, Ellie Yankton, Sarah Filburn, Amanirenas) and ten generic EncCitizenFaceM…_15 faces. Of the 277, 185 are voice-only placeholders (AUDIOONLY, NoAppearanceData); the rest include the 21 *_scan phenotype NPCs, the 16 ChargenHeadShapePreset_* NPCs and the Starborn faces. Children barely use tints at all: 43 of 50 ChildRace NPCs have no tint layer, and only 4 have Dermaesthetic.

    4.7 The four accent slots share two texture sets

    Accents1 and Accents2 are content-identical — 24 options each, same names, same texture paths, zero differences — held in separate, duplicated records (SimpleGroup_Accents1_color 0003CD42, SimpleGroup_Accents2_color 0003CD46). The duplication exists solely so a face can wear two accents at once: of 1,294 NPCs using both, all 1,294 pick different options in each.

    The Colorless variants go further — they are aliases onto the same records:

    ComplexGroup_Accents1           -> SimpleGroup_Accents1_{color,normal,rough,ao}
ComplexGroup_ColorlessAccents1  -> SimpleGroup_Accents1_{color,normal,rough,ao}   (identical)

    So two texture sets serve four slots. Nothing in the data distinguishes "colorless": all four slots have only a Complex definition (no Modulation palette), and every vanilla use of all four carries NNAM = 00000000. The distinction is in the engine, keyed on the slot name — and it is literal.

    "Colorless" means the colour channel is discarded (tested)

    ColorlessAccents1/2 ignore the _color texture's RGB and use only its ALPHA, as the coverage mask for the _normal/_rough/_ao detail. Accents1/2 apply the colour.

    Isolated in the Creation Kit, where only the slot changes. One vanilla option (Accents_UnderEyeBags_Small) had its _color texture replaced with opaque blue, then assigned to one slot and the other on the same NPC:

    slot baked face
    Accents1 blue — the colour texture is applied
    ColorlessAccents1 no blue — same texture, same NPC, same bake

    Confirmed a second time with a different option (Blemish_VeinsMany): in the CK it can be applied with or without colour by choosing the slot, and there is no way to apply it with colour from the in-game creator.

    In game it behaves the same way: a bright green _color texture on a colorless accent shows no green at all, and a texture whose alpha is opaque nearly everywhere spreads the accent's detail across the whole face — the alpha is acting as the mask, exactly as the slot name implies.

    ⚠️ A player can never wear a coloured accent. There is no Chargen_Accents1 or Chargen_Accents2 group, so the character creator reaches only the two Colorless slots (via Chargen_ColorlessAccents1/2). Accents1/2 are authoring-only — the CK and NPC records can use them, the in-game creator cannot.

    How vanilla works around it: it authors the same motif into several slots. The "many veins" blemish is the clearest case — it exists three times over, and what you can do with it depends entirely on which slot you reach it through:

    Offered as In A player gets
    Blemish_VeinsMany — _color/_normal/_rough/_ao Accents1/2, ColorlessAccents1/2 detail only; the creator reaches it via ColorlessAccents, so no colour
    Blemish_VeinsMany — the same four textures Scars (Chargen_Scars_*) colour and detail — the scar slot applies both
    Veins, Veins_2 — veins_mask.dds ComplexionMask1/2 ("Complexion Blemishes" in the UI), with dedicated Vein_Red and Vein_BlueGreen entries in the Modulation palette colour, tinted per choice, but no normal/rough/AO

    So the engine gives you colour or surface detail from these slots, not both — and the authors worked around it by duplicating the artwork into a slot with the other half. A custom race that needs both from one mark has the same options: duplicate it, or put it in Scars, which is the only creator-facing slot carrying all four channels.

    For a custom race the practical reading is which slots can carry multi-coloured marks at all:

    creator slot colour
    Dermaesthetic, Complexion1/2 full-colour texture
    Scars full-colour texture + normal/rough/ao
    Cheeks, Lipstick, Eyeshadow, Eyeliner, TattooMask, MakeupFullPaint greyscale mask × one Modulation colour
    ColorlessAccents1/2 none — alpha-masked normal/rough/ao detail only

    Scars shows the other Complex-group idiom — one slot fanning out to four same-named Simple groups (Scars_color/_normal/_rough/_ao, 25 options each), so a single layer buys four synchronised channels. 581 NPCs carry a scar; every one has exactly one.

    4.8 The stack is sex-agnostic

    Nothing in PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers distinguishes male from female — no per-gender blocks (unlike the RACE chargen payload, which repeats per sex), and the only sex marker is embedded in the phenotype strings. 24 of the 26 slots have no sex-specific content at all.

    The two exceptions both encode sex in option names inside a single shared group, not in the structure:

    • Dermaesthetic — all 84 options are sex-specific, in one group, and as above the engine does not enforce the match.
    • EnvironmentMask — the option names look neutral (Dirt_Light, Dirt_GroundHeavy) but every texture is test_dirt_female_*_mask.dds. The grime layer ships female-only placeholders applied to everyone.

    So per-sex overlay content is a naming convention an author maintains, not something the format supports or checks.

    4.9 Worked example — how the shipped Felid race uses this

    The published felid race is the useful precedent, because of what it doesn't do.

    • It never touches ComplexGroup_PostBlendFaceCustomizationLayers.
    • It repurposes the Lipstick2 slot for species face patterns. 23 of its 34 race-plugin NPCs use Lipstick2 and nothing else:
    NPC ChargenPresetMaleFelid_Leopard
   MNAM Simple   TNAM Lipstick2   QNAM LeopardFacePatterns_Default
   VNAM Textures/actors/Felid/faces/chargen/postblenddetails/FacePatterns/
        LeopardFacePattern_Default_color.dds
   NNAM ff000000   INTV 64
    • Its only vanilla overrides are surgical and additive — one entry (Stripe_Black) added to each of Modulation_Lipstick2 (40→41) and Modulation_Chargen_Lipstick2 (38→39), nothing removed. That override is unavoidable given §4.4: a paired Modulation group can't be referenced.
    • Everything else is new records under new names — Male_Naked_Body_Skin_Tones_Felid, FaceSkinTones_Felid, Eyebrows_Hair_Short. Its _AVMDEXT records claim their own distinct TNAMs, so they don't collide with the vanilla name index.
    • Its NPCs freely mix its own options with vanilla ones (Blush, Freckles_Nose, Eyeliner_Upper_02) resolved out of Starfield.esm.

    The takeaway for a custom race: work within the existing slot vocabulary. A five-species cat race needed no new slots, no layer-list edit, and two one-line overrides.

    Body shape is also stored per-NPC as MRSV Body Morph Region Values: five floats — Head / Upper Torso / Arms / Lower Torso / Legs — the runtime expression of the body triangle.

    4.10 The Chargen_* groups decide what the creator shows, and in what order

    A slot usually has two groups: the runtime one (ComplexionMask1) and a chargen-side twin (CHARGEN_ComplexionMask1). The in-game character creator reads the chargen-side group, and its record order is the order the options appear in the UI.

    Across the 20 slots that have both, 16 hold the same options in the same order — which is why the distinction stays invisible until it doesn't. The exceptions:

    slots effect
    re-ordered ComplexionMask1, ComplexionMask2 (46 options), TattooMask CHARGEN_ComplexionMask1 opens Freckles_Nose, Freckles_Forehead, Freckles_Cheek…, which sit at #21, #26 and #22 in the runtime group
    subset Eyeliner1, Eyeliner2 the creator offers 6 of the runtime 9 — three options exist but cannot be chosen
    absent Accents1, Accents2 no chargen-side group at all, so the creator cannot reach the slot (§4.7)

    ⚠️ The prefix is inconsistent in vanilla — CHARGEN_Cheeks1 and CHARGEN_ComplexionMask1 in caps vs Chargen_Lipstick2 and Chargen_Dermaesthetic. Every lookup here is by name, so a custom group must match the spelling the slot already uses.

    So the chargen-side group does three jobs: which options are offered, in what order, and — for dermaesthetic — filtered by skin tone, which is the next section.

    4.11 What in-game chargen offers

    Two different records decide what a slot shows in the character creator:

    • Dermaesthetic (Simple) contains skin tone overlays. They are named after the phenotype and skin tone options (<ethnicity>_<sex>_<age><n>_Sk<m>) but the skin tone options are not complete (1, 3, 6, 8) and the alpha lightens the degree to which they are applied.
    • Chargen_Dermaesthetic (Complex, [000D4004]) is the chargen filter: 9 entries index simple groups chargen_DermaestheticSkinTone0…8, one per skin-tone index -- BUT these simple groups are not simple maps to the skin tone. Instead, groups 0-4 select skin tones 1/3/6, whereas groups 5-8 select skin tones 3/6/8. Presumably the dermaesthetic overlay only looks good if it's not too different in tone from the underlying skin.

    NPC tint entries reference these names, and §4.1 shows the bake reads the stored VNAM path — so removing a vanilla entry here cannot break an existing NPC, but it does make that option unavailable for any NPC built afterwards.

    The in-game character creator offers the intersection. To restrict a slot to your own options, trim the filter, not the master list.

    ⚠️ The filter is keyed by tone index and shared by every race — there is no per-race variant. Whatever you put there, a human character created in the same load order sees it too. The same pattern holds for the head-part option lists (HeadPartsChargenOptionsMale [0003D9AF], 98 entries; HeadPartsChargenOptionsFemale [0003D9B0], 76) and for the preset gallery (CharGenPresets [0003F551]) — all global, all replaced wholesale rather than extended. A race mod that takes them over should ship that takeover in a separate player-facing plugin, the way Felid splits FelidRace.esm from FelidPlayer.esp, so the race can be installed without it.

    The CK validates that the filter is consistent with the master list — "all entries from skin tone filters are valid entries from the main Dermaesthetic reserved type" — so entries left in the filter that the master list no longer defines are a reported error.

    4.13 Overlay texture formats — uncompressed, and the header matters

    Face-customization layers are authored uncompressed. Census of all 448 vanilla files under textures/actors/human/faces/chargen/postblenddetails/, every one 1024×1024 with a single mip (as are all 274 base maps in faces/chargen/ itself; the size has to match across the set, below):

    suffix n format bytes
    _color 145 DX10 R8G8B8A8_UNORM_SRGB 4,194,452
    _mask 179 legacy header, uncompressed 8bpp red (R8) 1,048,704
    _ao 40 legacy 8bpp 1,048,704
    _rough 39 legacy 8bpp 1,048,704
    _normal 40 legacy uncompressed 32bpp signed 4,194,452
    *_mask_1 5 DX10 BC4_UNORM — the only compressed files here 524,436

    Bethesda's headers are also consistent, and differ from what a default texconv run produces. For the same pixels, vanilla writes:

    field vanilla texconv
    dwFlags 0xA1007 (…LINEARSIZE) 0x2100F (…PITCH)
    size field whole image (w×h×bpp) one row's pitch
    dwDepth 0 1
    dwCaps 0x401008 (COMPLEX/TEXTURE/MIPMAP) 0x1000 (TEXTURE)
    DX10 alphaMode 0 (unknown) 1 (straight)
    8bpp pixel-format flags DDPF_RGB (0x40) DDPF_LUMINANCE (0x20000)

    Both encodings are legal DDS. They are called out because a mod author reports the creator's dermaesthetic chooser rejecting layers that were the right format but carried texconv's header; matching vanilla's fields is the cheap way to take the question off the table. texconv needs -m 1 to stop building a mip chain, and no -dx10 for the 8bpp masks, since vanilla writes those with a legacy header.

    Every face texture must be the same size — any size (in game, 2026-10-18). The creator re-composites the face each time a tint layer changes, from the race's FCTP base maps (<phenotype>_sk<N>_color, _normal, _rough, _ao, the FCT_<region>_masks) and the chosen options. A custom race whose files were at mixed sizes (colour 4096, normal 1024, rough 2048, overlays 4096) looked right until the first dermaesthetic change, then rendered the face black, for both sexes. With every file at 1024 it worked; with every file at 2048 it worked too. So the rule is "one size", not "1024": vanilla's are all 1024 only because they all match.

    The consequence for a race that goes bigger than 1024: every vanilla option the creator still offers it is 1024: scars, accents, lipstick, the second complexion and so on. Picking any of them would black the face. So either keep the face set at 1024, or replace or empty every tint group the creator shows that race.

    Layers composite by the texture's own alpha — there is no per-slot blend mode doing it for you. A layer with opaque alpha lands flat on top and hides what is beneath. Vanilla's dermaesthetic layers are correspondingly soft: in a sampled file only 63 pixels of a million exceed alpha 250, and the commonest non-zero values are 1 and 17. They also keep real skin colour in RGB underneath transparent areas, so bilinear filtering never bleeds a halo at an edge — worth copying, since transparent-white padding will fringe.

    5. The body & head mesh — .mesh files and BSGeometry

    The biggest structural change from [FO4]/[Skyrim]: geometry is no longer stored inside the .nif.

    The NIF keeps the scene graph and the skinning; the vertices live in a separate .mesh file it points at. meshes.md is the reference for all of it — the BSGeometry block, the geometries/ path scheme, the .mesh byte layout, and where bone names and bind transforms are stored. This page covers only what bites you on a head part.

    Practical gotcha: the community reports "internal geometry" (embedding mesh data in the NIF rather than an external .mesh) causes issues with head parts — head parts should reference external .mesh files.

    Watch the mesh-path length. The "creating a playable race" guide reports that the path stored in BSGeometry → Meshes must be ≤ 46 characters or the shape renders invisible in game while looking correct in the Creation Kit — see meshes.md. Reported, not measured here. Head parts are where it would bite first, since a race-namespaced folder plus a part name eats the budget fast; keep them short regardless.

    Body layout

    The body is delivered by the race's WNAM Skin ARMO and its armor addons, cut into biped slots (§7). Community body replacers (e.g. the SFF female body) show the division as separate meshes for body, hands, feet, first-person body/hands, head, each skinned to the shared skeleton, each with its own .mesh, morphs, UVs, and skin-tone textures (sk6/sk7/sk8 texture variants seen in the wild). Head and body are separate meshes joined at the neck.

    Tools: NifSkope (fo76utils fork) for Starfield NIFs; Starfield Geometry Bridge / StarfieldMeshConverter (Blender) for .mesh and .morph I/O. [PyNifly] — nifly's BSGeometry support is the C++ basis for reading these.

    6. Skeleton, and adding a tail / ears / digitigrade legs

    The skeleton

    Still a NIF-based skeleton.nif referenced by RACE → Skeleton Data → ANAM Skeletal Model. Bone naming uses a C_ (center) / L_ / R_ prefix convention with a Root and COM (centre-of-mass) at the top, then C_Hips, spine, neck, head, and limb chains. (Contrast [Skyrim] NPC Root [Root] / NPC COM [COM] and [FO4] Root/COM/Pelvis — same idea, new names.)

    Verified against the extracted vanilla meshes/actors/human/characterassets/skeleton.nif (Gamebryo 20.2.0.7, BSVersion 173): 116 NiNode blocks. The community HumanRace.json's "92 bones" counts only the deform bones and omits the ~24 helper nodes present in the file (cameras, AnimObject*, IK/target helpers, physics nodes). The full ordered list, grouped:

    • Roots/helpers: HumanExportRoot, Root, COM, CamTargetParent, CameraTarget, Camera Control, Camera Control FP, Camera, AnimObjectA–D, Eye_Target, DirectAt, WEAPON, WeaponLeft, R_HandIk, L_HandIk, CharacterBumper, CharacterController (+ one unnamed helper node).
    • Spine/head: C_Hips, C_Spine, C_Spine1, C_Spine2, C_Chest, C_Neck, C_Neck1, C_Neck_Twist, C_Head, L_Eye, R_Eye.
    • Legs (mirrored L_/R_): Thigh, Calf, Foot, Toe, CalfMass, Knee, Thigh_Twist, Thigh_Twist1, Butt.
    • Arms (mirrored): Clavicle, Biceps, Forearm, Wrist, Arm, Deltoid, Elbow, ArmMass, Biceps_Twist, Biceps_Twist1, Wrist_Twist, Wrist_Twist1, Wrist_Twist2, AnimObject1–3.
    • Hands (mirrored): Cup, Thumb/1/2, Index/1/2, Middle/1/2, Ring/1/2, Pinky/1/2.
    • Misc: C_BackPack, C_BackPackHose.

    Convention confirmed: C_ = centerline, L_/R_ = mirrored, suffixes _Twist[N] / …Mass / …Ik. The skinned body NIF weights to a 38-bone subset of these (via SkinAttach, see §5). The community SF Extended Skeleton mod adds extra bones (tail, ears, breast, belly, genitalia…) on top of this base and is the de-facto dependency for body mods that need new bones.

    Two ways to add animated appendages

    (a) Bone Modifiers (BMOD) — the Starfield-native, no-behavior-graph path. A race lists BMOD records under Head Parts and Bone Modifiers → Bone Modifiers. Each BMOD has a DATA struct: 

    Type    : LookAtChain | MorphDriver | PoseDeformer | SpringBone
Driver  : source bone/node
Target  : bone to modify
Max Anim Distance
+ type-dependent data
    - SpringBone — jiggle/physics chain (Strength, Damp, Scale, MaxDist, Look At Parent). This is how a tail or ears can be made to swing at runtime without authoring a Havok behavior graph. - LookAtChain — chain that aims at a target (ears tracking, tail follow). - PoseDeformer — corrective pose-driven deformation (position/angle/scale + falloff radius + axis). - MorphDriver — drives a morph from bone motion (vector + falloff radius).

    Because BMODs attach at the RACE level, a furry race can — in principle — add a physics tail and mobile ears by (1) adding the bones to skeleton.nif, (2) skinning the tail/ear .mesh to those bones, and (3) adding SpringBone/ LookAtChain BMODs — all in data, no behavior-graph compile step. That would be the single biggest practical advantage Starfield has over [Skyrim]/[FO4] for appendages (which needed XPMSSE + HDT/CBP or custom behavior work).

    Conflicting evidence — verify before relying on this. The BMOD SpringBone mechanism is confirmed to exist in the RACE record definition, but the shipped community appendages tell a more cautious story: the Felid race's tail is driven by Havok cloth physics (a low-poly sim mesh weighted to the tail bones, baked with BSClothExtraData via the Havok Content Tools), and is widely reported as "wonky." Separately, the SF Extended Skeleton documentation states its added bones "do not have their own collisions" and are intended for cloth physics, and one modder resource states plainly that "additional bones cannot be controlled" by the game. It is not yet confirmed that any shipped mod drives custom appendages through vanilla BMOD SpringBone rather than the cloth route. Treat "tail/ears via BMOD" as a promising but unproven path and settle it with a throwaway in-engine test early — the fallback is Havok cloth (proven, imperfect).

    (b) Full behavior graph. For animated (keyframed, gameplay-reactive) appendages you still need the Havok behavior graph (RACE → Subgraph Data → SGNM Behaviour Graph + animation paths). There is no public Starfield behavior graph editor (hkbProject authoring), so extending the behavior graph is a hard blocker — most non-human mods reuse HumanRace's graph and rely on BMODs + new geometry rather than new locomotion.

    The skin ARMO chain (WNAM → ARMO → ARMA)

    A head part is named by the race directly; the body is three hops away. The race's WNAM names a skin ARMO, whose repeated INDX+MODL pairs name ARMA armor addons — one per biped slot:

    ARMA field Meaning
    RNAM the race the addon fits
    MODL (repeated) Additional Races. ⚠ On an ARMO the same signature is the addon list instead — same four letters, unrelated payloads.
    MOD2 / MOD3 male / female 3rd-person NIF
    MOD4 / MOD5 male / female 1st-person NIF
    NAM4 / NAM5 / NAM6 / NAM7 the MRPH body morph paired with each of those four models
    BO64 biped slot mask — body 0x08, hands 0x40
    record flags 0x80 Is Skin, 0x100 Is Skin Hands, 0x400 No Model

    Vanilla human skin is Skin_Naked → AA_Naked_Body + AA_Naked_Hands. The ARMO's own BO64 is 0x08 only and does not need to cover its addons' slots. The frozen-corpse skin addons carry neither Is Skin flag, so a skin addon doesn't need one.

    But the flag decides the addon's colour (in game, 2026-10-14 and 10-18). An addon flagged Is Skin is painted with the race's body tone texture (BSTT) by the actor's STON; one flagged Is Skin Hands gets the hands tone (HSTT). Either way the texture its own material names is overridden. Two ways this bites:

    • A tail flagged Is Skin Hands got the hands texture at body UVs: the wrong colour everywhere. A tail is skin, so flag it Is Skin.
    • Crowd tails copied from a skin addon, each with its own blue material, came out red: the copies kept the flag, so the engine coloured them from the tone ladder and ignored the material. An addon that carries its own colour should have the flag cleared. (That clearing the flag fixes it is the next in-game check.)

    ⚠ An addon whose race list excludes the actor's race is skipped without a word — that slot of the body simply does not render. This is the easiest way to end up with an invisible body or missing hands on a custom race.

    RACE\RNAM "Armor Race" complicates the match. A custom race normally points it at HumanRace so vanilla clothing fits, which leaves its own skin addons with two candidate races — and vanilla does not settle which the engine matches on:

    race Armor Race its skin ARMA names
    MannequinRace HumanRace MannequinRace only
    HumanCorpseRace HumanRace HumanRace (+ Corpse, Mannequin as additional)

    Mannequins work only under the actual-race rule; corpses work under either. HumanRace itself leaves Armor Race unset. Practical rule: list both — it costs nothing, and it is the first thing to check on an invisible body.

    Body morphs are indexed by vertex, exactly like the head. NAM4/NAM6 point at MRPH records whose morph.dat must match the model's vertex count — MORPH_Human_Male_Body is 6,464 verts, 3 keys (the Overweight/Thin/Strong body-build morphs, which are engine body-build morphs and not members of the race's MPGM list). A body remeshed to a different vertex count cannot use them.

    An NPC can override the race's skin outright. NPC_ carries its own WNAM Skin (ARMO), used by 1,851 NPCs in Starfield.esm, 147 in ShatteredSpace.esm and 36 in SFBGS00D.esm. This is the only mechanism that puts a genuinely different body — rather than a different tone — on an actor of a given race. ⚠ It is authored per record, so it serves hand-built NPCs but cannot be driven by chargen: a playable race cannot offer it as a player choice.

    8. FaceGen (baking NPC faces)

    [Skyrim]/[FO4] bake a per-NPC head into meshes/actors/character/facegendata/facegeom/<Plugin>/<FormID>.nif plus a face-tint .dds. Starfield keeps the concept: the Creation Kit bakes each NPC's head part + morph-dial values + tint layers into a per-NPC head mesh and tint texture written under Data/… (loose, then archived).

    • Verified: the CK regenerates faces per-NPC (community "Regenerating Faces" guides exist); a NPCs Face Data Synthesis patcher fixes missing masters so the CK loads the right resources; and failed/mismatched facegen produces the classic dark-face / neck-seam skin-tone mismatch and floating eyes/teeth.
    • Resolved (2026-07): the Starfield output layout is not FO4's facegendata/facegeom. The per-race base head is baked to a texture set at Data\Textures\actors\Character\FaceCustomization\<Plugin>.esp\BaseHead<RaceEDID><Sex>_<map>.dds, keyed by the race EDID — or …\<NPC FormID>_<map>.dds once the NPC has at least one tint layer. Full details, formats, and the CK/CKPE baking workflow are on the Creation Kit & FaceGen page.
    • The bake never reads the material — but the material still drives the face at render time. Keep the two apart, because the same .dds files are involved in both:
      • What the bake reads: all four maps come from the race's FCTP directory by filename convention (§1, FCTP) — albedo, normal, roughness and AO. Measured with the loose male_default.mat deliberately pointed at unrelated textures, the baked _rough and _ao came out byte-identical to male_default_rough.dds / male_default_ao.dds and _Normal identical within BC5 quantisation. The material's texture paths are never consulted. The albedo is optionally redirected per skin tone through the FSTT → AVMD chain (§4); normal, roughness and AO have no AVMD route.
      • Why it looks like the material supplies them: a head material's base layer points at those very same FCTP files. The bake reaches them by filename, not by following the material, so the two agree until you change one without the other.
      • What the material still contributes: its upper layers are not baked in and composite at render time instead — a vanilla head material carries five detail-normal layers (pore, stubble, lips, cheek, skin detail), each masked to a face region. So the per-region normal detail across the face is the material's doing; it simply never reaches the baked maps. (That last step is inference, not measurement — see the note in Creation Kit §2.)

    See Creation Kit §2–3.

    Implication for a furry race's NPCs: every NPC of the race must be baked in the CK with the race's head parts, morphs, and fur tints — a bulk, error-prone step. This is exactly the pain point that the furrifier tooling (writing the baked .nif/.dds directly instead of Ctrl+F4-ing in the CK) aims to eliminate.

    9. Building a custom race

    A truly new playable race — own RACE record, own skeleton, own behavior graph — is not a solved problem. Every shipped "anthro/beast" race is a skinned reworking of HumanRace: new head parts, new tint groups, new body/head .mesh + .morph, BMOD-driven appendages, reusing the human skeleton and behavior graph. Plan for that shape of solution.

    What follows is a realistic build order, easiest→hardest. Each step lists what it produces, so this doubles as the completeness checklist.

    1. Clone HumanRace (or override it) rather than authoring a new skeleton/behavior graph — you inherit locomotion, facegen wiring and biped slots for free. Produces: the RACE record, with Skeleton Data (ANAM/NAM5/NAM6/DNAM), WNAM Skin ARMO, GNAM Body Part Data and the FaceGen clamps set.
    2. Skeleton. The human skeleton, or SF Extended Skeleton, plus tail/ear bones if you want animated appendages. Produces: skeleton.nif.
    3. Body & head meshes. Model in Blender; export with PyNifly or Starfield Geometry Bridge, skinned to that skeleton. Starfield heads carry a much higher vert count than older games, so there is room to work. Consider both the normal and the SS (spacesuit) slots. Produces: body/head/hands/feet .mesh files and their NIFs (BSGeometry + SkinAttach). Keep mesh paths short (§5).
    4. Morphs. Author performance (expression) and chargen shape keys for the head, and "Overweight" / "Strong" / "Thin" for the body. Produces: .morph files, MRPH records, and optionally a bone-morph definition.
    5. Head parts. Create HDPT records for head, mouth/teeth, tongue, eyes — and the Creature Head / Tail / Wings / Arms / Legs types for muzzle, ears and tail. Set RNAM Valid Races to your race's FLST, point MNAM at the MRPH, and wire the defaults into RACE → Head Parts. Produces: the HDPT set and its FLST.
    6. Fur / markings. Build AVMD tint groups (stripes, spots, muzzle mask, belly) — either overriding vanilla groups or extending them, since slots resolve by name (§4.4, §4.11). Reference them from RACE → Chargen and Skintones and head parts' Color Mapping. Layers stack, so patterns are additive overlays. Produces: AVMD/AVMS groups, skin-tone subtypes, and the TXST/face textures behind them.
    7. Chargen visibility. Append your head parts to HeadPartsChargenOptionsMale [FLST:0003D9AF] / …Female [0003D9B0]. Eye Color needs both a left- and a right-eye head part. This list is one of several things the engine finds by hardcoded FormID rather than through your RACE record — see §1, What the RACE record does not reach for the consolidated set.
    8. Creator sliders. Expose custom sliders via CharGenMenu + JSON whose keys match your .morph keys; define Face Dials and Face Morph Phenotypes in the RACE record for presets. Produces: the chargen wiring, plus BMOD Bone Modifiers (SpringBone for tail/ear jiggle, LookAtChain for ear tracking) on RACE → Bone Modifiers, and BPTD Body Part Data if you changed the anatomy's dismemberment regions.
    9. FaceGen bake. Bake every NPC of the race in the CK; check the neck seam's skin-tone match and that eyes and teeth are not floating. Consider a direct facegen writer rather than mass Ctrl+F4.
    10. Player plugin. Put the race in one plugin and the player overrides in a second, so the race can be present for NPC mods without forcing you to play as it. See "The player records" below.
    11. Ship it. Pack as <Name> - Textures.ba2 plus <Name> - Main.ba2; " - Meshes.ba2" is no longer recognised by Starfield. Players will also need SFSE, CharGenMenu, and likely SF Extended Skeleton.

    The player records

    Finishing fixes & gotchas

    Concrete FormIDs/steps that trip up first-time race authors:

    • Spacesuit not showing: add your race to HumanRaceORChildRaceORHumanCorpseRace [CNDF:0003D8C1].
    • Dialogue camera too close: add your race to DC_Race_Human [CPTH:000C1056].
    • Invisible limbs in new-game chargen: give Clothes_GenWare_01 "Space Undersuit" [ARMO:00165722] a skin ARMA / remodel it to your naked-skin model.
    • Eyebrows fully transparent: they also need SimpleGroup_EyebrowOpacity [AVMD:0000EF9F] and NAM3 - Mask = EyebrowColor on the head part.
    • Quirks: the first eyebrow in a list ignores its assigned texture (engine bug).

    Hard blockers

    • Behavior graph. No public Starfield behavior-graph editor → no new locomotion; reuse human legs.
    • Format maturity. .mesh/.morph are reverse-engineered, not documented by Bethesda; tooling (SGB/StarfieldMeshConverter/PyNifly) is the source of truth and can lag engine updates.
    • FaceGen scale. Baking many NPCs is slow and fragile (dark-face bug).
    • Creator UI. Radically non-human morphology stresses a UI/morph system designed around a human face; sliders assume human topology.
    • True new RACE. Own skeleton + behavior graph + genome integration is unproven; the safe route is a HumanRace variant.

    Open questions / uncertainties

    1. BMOD limits — how many SpringBone chains the engine handles gracefully, and whether ear LookAtChain works on first-person, is untested here.
    2. Can a genuinely new RACE (non-HumanRace-parented) enter the creator? No confirmed example; all anthro results are HumanRace variants.
    3. Digitigrade locomotion — no confirmed shipped example; behavior-graph gap is the suspected blocker.
    4. Does the player's live face use stored tint paths, like the NPC bake? The bake is proven to use each tint entry's stored VNAM (§4.1). Whether the player's creator-driven composite re-reads the AVMD groups, and whether layer order comes from the stack (§4.3), is untested.
