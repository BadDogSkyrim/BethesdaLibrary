    Starfield Crowds

    City crowds are ordinary NPC records on a lightweight race, spawned by region records and dressed by their outfits. The head comes from the outfit too: one baked mesh per head, with the eyes and both arms merged in. This page covers how the pieces fit, measured from Starfield.esm and the unpacked vanilla assets, and what a crowd of a new species needs, from building one (a fox crowd, tested in game October 2026).

    The chargen page has the overview; this page has the detail.

    1. How crowds spawn

    Crowds come from 19 CrowdRegion_* records (signature GBFM, carrying a BGSCrowdComponent). Each holds a density (CDND), a population count (CDNS), and one or more populations, typically a day and a night one, each with:

    • conditions (CITC + CTDA), which is how day/night is chosen;
    • a name (STRV: CrowdCompDayTime, CrowdCompNightTime) and a scale (FLTV);
    • a proportions list (PRPS): entries of {actor, weight, curve table}. Vanilla weights are 100 or 50.

    A proportions entry must be an NPC_; xEdit's definition allows no leveled NPC list. So the variety comes from the NPC's outfit, not from the spawn list.

    The CrowdActor_* NPCs are also used by 28 dialogue conditions, 3 quests and a few cell placements. Changing their race changes those too; adding new NPCs to the proportions lists avoids that.

    2. The crowd actors

    Census of the 138 CrowdActor_* NPCs (excluding the _TEMPLATE, _VOICE and _KEYWORDS helpers), 2026-10-16:

    Field Value
    Race (RNAM) HumanCrowdRace [002BBC09], all 138
    Skin (WNAM) Skin_Crowd_Base_Body_NOTPLAYABLE, the race's own skin
    Skin tone (STON) present on all 138
    Head parts, face morphs, tint layers none
    Default outfit (DOFT) Outfit_Crowds_<district>_<sex>
    Template flags (ACBS, u16 at offset 14) Factions + Keywords (0x1004) on 95; Traits + Factions + Keywords (0x1005) on 24; other combinations on 19

    Crowds have no skin tones. HumanCrowdRace names no skin-tone groups at all (no BSTT, HSTT or FSTT), and 176 of 181 crowd actors set STON to 0. Every visible bit of skin is inside the one head mesh with its own baked texture, so head, neck and hands always match. Anything else that has to match the head, like a tail, has to come in the same tone by construction (§5).

    Traits carries race. The 24 that inherit Traits take them from LCharCrowdVoices_Male / _Female, whose 10 entries are all HumanCrowdRace NPCs. Copy one of those actors, set a new race, and the template quietly sets it back. Turn the Traits flag off (and zero TPTA entry 0) on a copy that needs its own race.

    The outfit

    An outfit holds three leveled lists: clothes (biped slot 3), a head list (slot 2), and a hair-or-hat list (slot 1). Across the 39 crowd outfits there are 15 pairings: 3 head lists (LL_Crowd_Heads, _Thin, _Overweight) × 5 hair lists (NoHats, Hats_ALL, Hats_Akila, Hats_NA, Hats_Neon). The 168 vanilla crowd armors use only slots 1–3.

    Each head list holds 8 head armors, CrowdHead_<phenotype>_NOTPLAYABLE, one per human phenotype, shared by both sexes. Each armor's single addon names a male NIF (MOD2), a female NIF (MOD3), and one shared performance morph per sex (NAM4 / NAM6 → an MRPH under Meshes\Morphs\Human_Crowd\<Sex>\Performance).

    3. The crowd head

    A crowd head NIF is one BSGeometry: head, eyes, mouth and both arms from the biceps to the fingertips, merged into one mesh. The crowd race's skin is itself clothing (AA_Clothes_Crowd_Sweater_01_NOTPLAYABLE, no bare skin), so the hands come with the head.

    • Size: 4,179 vertices (female), 4,302 (male). Every crowd head of a sex shares that topology, which is what lets one performance morph (11 keys: jawOpen, blinks, brows, lips) serve all of them.
    • Skin: 52–55 bones: head, neck, both eyes, both arms.
    • Material: one Materials\Actors\Human\Crowd\*.mat with baked colour, normal, rough, metal and AO textures. Crowd heads have no skin tones: a colour variation is a separate texture set.
    • LOD: 4 slots over 3 .mesh files, ABBC (meshes §1).
    • Eye positions: a root BoneTranslations block, BoneTrn, holding where this head's eyes are. Without it the eyes are drawn in the wrong place; see meshes: BoneTranslations.
    • Body types: every head has _thin and _overweight copies (no suffix = average), each with its own _facebones companion, so the arms fit that body type's sleeves.

    The _facebones companion is just a copy

    Every crowd head has a <name>_facebones.nif. Unlike a chargen head's companion, it has no face bones: the same bones, shape and material as the plain NIF, and the same slot-0 .mesh, with only slot 0 listed. The crowd skeleton_facebones.nif likewise equals skeleton.nif (only DirectAt differs). There is no face rig on a crowd actor.

    This matters (verified in game): the game draws a crowd head from its companion. A companion skinned to faceBone_* bones, the way a chargen head's is, renders an invisible head, because the crowd skeleton has none of those bones. Write the companion as a copy of the plain NIF.

    4. The crowd skeleton

    HumanCrowdRace uses its own skeleton NIF (Actors\human_crowd\CharacterAssets\skeleton.nif, and \Female\) but the vanilla human skeleton.rig and the same Human_Root.agx animation graph as HumanRace. There is no crowd .rig.

    Compared with the human skeleton:

    • 90 nodes against 115, all at the same positions, none reparented.
    • 9 twist bones are rotated differently: C_Neck_Twist, L/R_Biceps_Twist, L/R_Wrist_Twist1/2, L/R_Thigh_Twist, by 29–152°.
    • No BSConnectPoint::Parents on the root, and no C_BackPack ragdoll body.

    Nothing is bound to those twist rotations. Measured over all 144 vanilla crowd clothing NIFs (both sexes, all three body types), 1,112 twist-bone binds: 0 match the crowd skeleton, 1,076 match the human skeleton exactly, and 36 (the female Neon rainwear's arms) sit 3.6° from human against 30–143° from crowd. The crowd heads bind to the human orientations too. That's why crowd outfits look warped in the Creation Kit until a pose is applied: the unposed crowd skeleton has the odd rotations, and a pose overrides them.

    So bind new crowd meshes to the human skeleton's orientations, as vanilla's are.

    5. A crowd of another species

    What was built and tested for a fox crowd (October 2026), and what's still open.

    Worked in game:

    • A crowd race of its own (copied from HumanCrowdRace) with new NPCs copied from crowd actors and set to it. Inherited Traits must be turned off (§2).
    • The head as a head armor in the NPC's outfit, on a new head list. No vanilla armor or list is touched.
    • The _facebones companion as a plain copy (§3).
    • BoneTrn copied from a vanilla head when the custom head's eyes sit where that head's do. The eye bones must be at the eyeball centres, or the eyes wobble as they turn.
    • Colour variants as separate texture sets, each with its own .mat copied from the head's material and its own copy of the NIF pointing at it. All variants share one .mesh, morph and BoneTrn. The NIF names its material twice: a header string and the shape's MaterialID (a CRC-32 of the path; see materials). Both must change.
    • Variant materials need their own object ids. Each copy keeps the res: namespace (words 2–3) and gets new first words for the objects it defines, which is how vanilla's crowd materials differ from each other. References into imported templates keep theirs. Variants made that way render distinct textures in game.
    • A full skeleton on the crowd race. The race's ANAMs pointed at copies of Extended Skeleton's human skeletons (which add a tail chain), under the race's own path. Vanilla crowd clothes render correctly on it, as §4's bind census predicts.
    • No WNAM on the NPCs. An NPC's own skin overrides its race's, and the vanilla crowd actors carry one (the human crowd skin). Copies that keep it never wear the new race's skin; drop the field and give an NPC a skin only when it should differ from its race.
    • A tail renders on the crowd foxes. It came from the race's skin: the player race's tail addon (body slot 15, with its BSClothExtraData) with the crowd race added to its races.

    Not yet tested:

    • A tail in each head's tone. Since crowds have no skin tones (§2), a tail on the race skin is one colour on every actor. Built: one tail addon per tone, each with its own material, carried by the head armor as a second addon (the armor's BO64 gains slot 15), so the leveled list's pick brings the matching tail. The first try came out red because the copies kept the source addon's Is Skin flag (chargen); the flag is cleared now and awaits a look in game.
    • Whether crowd actors simulate the tail's cloth at the same distances as full NPCs.
    • The alternative of adding species addons to the vanilla head and hair armors, relying on armor race to pick the addon.
