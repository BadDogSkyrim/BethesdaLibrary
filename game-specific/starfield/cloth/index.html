    Starfield Cloth (BSClothExtraData)

    Hair, tails, skirts, coat flaps, capes and banners move by Havok cloth. The simulation lives in a nif's BSClothExtraData block. This page covers the block's format, how a cloth is put together, what the game does with it, and what we learned building cloth from scratch and testing it in game.

    Measured on all 298 vanilla nifs with cloth (320 pieces of cloth), October 2026. In-game results are from a fox tail, the vanilla ponytail and a vanilla skirt. PyNifly reads, edits and builds cloth (30.1.0); its user page covers the Blender side.

    1. What cloth does

    Cloth only moves bones. It simulates a small set of particles: a strip down a ponytail, a few dozen across a skirt. Their triangles drive bones, and the render mesh is skinned to those bones the ordinary way. Nothing in the blob refers to the render mesh.

    • Pinned particles (mass 0) follow the skeleton. The free ones are simulated.
    • Each driven bone follows one sim triangle.
    • Collision shapes (capsules, tapered capsules, spheres) ride body bones and keep particles out of the body.
    • Constraints hold the particles in shape: links, hinges, distance limits and bone planes.

    2. The container: a Havok 2019 tagfile

    The payload is a Havok tagfile: magic TAG0, SDK 20190200. [FO4] used an older Havok packfile with its type information stripped. The Starfield file carries its own type definitions: every class, field name, offset and size. So a generic reader turns it into named objects without any class definitions supplied.

    Each section header is a big-endian uint32 and a 4-character tag. The top two bits of the uint32 say whether the section holds other sections (0) or data (1); the low 30 bits give its size, including the 8-byte header.

    TAG0
  SDKV   "20190200"
  DATA   the object bytes
  TYPE
    TPTR   runtime pointer table (zeros in a file)
    TST1   type-name strings, NUL-separated
    TNA1   the types: name + template parameters
    FST1   field-name strings, NUL-separated
    TBDY   each type's parent, format, size/alignment and fields
    THSH   type hashes
    TPAD   padding
  INDX
    ITEM   the item table: {u32 type+kind, u32 offset into DATA, u32 count}; item 0 is null
    PTCH   where in DATA the references between items sit

    Reading the types:

    • Integers in TNA1/TBDY are Havok's variable-length form, big-endian. Below 0x80 is one byte; top bits 10 add a byte (14-bit value); 110 take three bytes, and so on.
    • TNA1: a count (including type 0), then per type a name index into TST1 and its template parameters. A parameter whose name starts with t holds a type; one starting with v holds an integer.
    • TBDY: per type, its index, its parent, and an option mask saying what follows. 0x01 format, 0x02 element type, 0x04 version, 0x08 size and alignment, 0x10 flags, 0x20 fields, 0x40 interfaces, 0x80 attribute.
    • A field is name (an FST1 index), flags, offset and type. When the flags have bit 0x80 set, one extra value comes before the offset. In vanilla only hkPackedVector3.values and hclCollidable.userData do this; its meaning is unknown.
    • The format's low 5 bits give the kind: 2 bool, 3 string, 4 int (bit 0x200 signed), 5 float, 6 pointer, 7 class, 8 array. An array with bit 0x20 set is inline, its length in the bits above 8: hkVector4 is 0x428 (four floats), hkMatrix4 is 0x1028 (sixteen).
    • In DATA, a pointer, a string or an ordinary array is a uint32 item index.
    • PTCH is grouped by the type of the reference field: u32 type, u32 count, then that many DATA-relative offsets. Null references aren't listed.
    • Some blobs name a type without defining it (no TBDY body). An empty array of such a type needs no element size.

    Writing one: two traps

    Each of these makes Starfield drop the cloth silently: the mesh renders, but it doesn't move.

    1. Every PTCH offset must lie inside an item. A reference left in a gap of DATA kills the cloth.
    2. Every TYPE subsection must be padded to 4 bytes: TST1 and FST1 with 0xFF, the others with zeros. Zero padding at the end of TBDY reads as empty entries, so anything after it is never read.

    Not required, all tested in game: item order, THSH order, body order, section alignment, and unused gaps in DATA.

    3. How a cloth is put together

    Every vanilla cloth has the same structure. A blob holds a hkRootLevelContainer whose variants are one hclClothData per piece of cloth (the pirate officer's lower half has six) and one hkaSkeleton.

    Per hclClothData:

    Part Holds
    simClothDatas[1] the particles, sim triangles, constraint sets and collidables (hclSimClothData)
    bufferDefinitions[2] a scratch buffer and the sim buffer, one vertex per particle
    transformSetDefinitions[1] the skeleton's bones
    operators skin, move particles, simulate, bone deform (once or twice), copy
    clothStateDatas[2] Simulate = skin, move, simulate, deform; Animate = skin, copy, deform
    • Skin (hclBoneSpaceSkinPNOperator or hclObjectSpaceSkinPNOperator) places every particle from the skeleton. Pinned particles are then held there by move particles. Free ones use it as the anchor for local range, and the Animate state puts them there outright.
    • Bone deform (hclSimpleMeshBoneDeformOperator) drives the bones.
    • Copy (hclCopyVerticesOperator or hclGatherAllVerticesOperator) feeds Animate.

    Bone-space skinning (vanilla hair uses it) stores particles in blocks:

    Bones per particle Particles per block
    4 4
    2 8
    1 16
    • Slot k*n + j holds bone j of the block's particle k, where n is the bones per particle.
    • localPosition is (w · B⁻¹p, w): the weight times the particle in the bone's bind frame.
    • Normals are packed int16 (value / 32768, exponent word 12288).

    Each driven bone follows its nearest triangle: the one whose centre is nearest the bone (5,736 of 5,742 vanilla pairs). Its offset is L = F⁻¹ · B_bind, where:

    • B_bind is the bone's bind matrix;
    • F is the triangle's frame, with columns [p0 − c, p1 − c, (p0 − c) × (p1 − c), c] (c the centre; the cross product not normalised).

    The skeleton

    The blob carries its own hkaSkeleton, and its reference pose is the bind the cloth uses. Vanilla skeletons range from 2 bones to the whole authoring scene: the ponytail carries 165, including face bones; 27 cloths carry 5 and 11 carry 2. A skeleton of just the bones the cloth uses works. We tested the used bones plus their ancestors up to Root.

    Bones are matched by name. A cloth's own bones don't need to exist anywhere else:

    • The ponytail's C_Cloth_PonyTail_00–_04 are in no skeleton nif, and not among the hair nif's nodes. They exist only in the blob's skeleton (which gives their parent and rest pose) and by name in the mesh's skin.
    • You can make up cloth bone names. A fox tail rebuilt with invented bones C_Cloth_Tail_00–_08 worked in game. Give them sensible parents (the tail's first bone under C_Hips).

    Cloth bones in a skinned nif

    A vanilla Starfield skin names its bones in SkinAttach, one per slot, and its BSSkin::Instance.boneRefs are empty. A shape weighted to cloth bones can also point some slots at NiNodes. Then:

    • The node names the slot. SkinAttach holds the cloth bone's parent in that slot (the Collector coat's slots say C_Chest and C_Hips; the nodes say R_Cloth_Jacket and so on).
    • The weights and binds still index every slot (37 for the Collector's upper body, 11 of them nodes). A reader that takes only the non-empty node refs gets the wrong bone for every weight.
    • The nodes are flattened under the root, each holding the bone's transform relative to its real parent, in metres. They are no use as a pose; the bind is where the bone is.
    • The Collector's upper body also has a non-identity BSGeometry transform: C_Head's position, in metres. That's against the rule in Meshes that a skinned shape's transform is identity.

    26 vanilla shapes use node slots, including the Collector outfit, a farmer outfit and a pirate upper body. A shape weighted only to made-up cloth bones, with plain SkinAttach names and no nodes, also works in game.

    4. Constraints

    Each hclSimClothData lists its constraint sets in staticConstraintSets. The simulate operator's constraintExecution gives the order. Vanilla always runs them in this order:

    1. local range
    2. bone planes
    3. standard links and compressible links
    4. bend stiffness
    5. stretch links
    6. volume
    Set Cloths (of 320) What it does
    Standard links (hclStandardLinkConstraintSetMx) 277 keep two particles at their rest distance
    Stretch links (hclStretchLinkConstraintSetMx) 263 stop two particles getting further apart; one end is pinned
    Local range (hclLocalRangeConstraintSet) 233 limit how far a particle strays from its skinned position
    Bend stiffness (hclBendStiffnessConstraintSetMx) 128 a hinge spring at each edge two triangles share
    Bone planes (hclBonePlanesConstraintSet) 57 keep a particle on one side of a plane riding a bone
    Compressible links 43
    Volume 7

    Records come as singles and as batches of four in parallel arrays.

    Standard links. Vanilla gives every link in a set one stiffness s, split by inverse mass: stiffnessA = s·wA/(wA+wB), stiffnessB = −s·wB/(wA+wB). A link between two pinned particles does nothing.

    Stretch links. restLength is the distance times a slack factor: 1.0 in most cloths, up to 1.43. Vanilla runs them from the pinned root to the tip, so the whole length can't stretch.

    Bend stiffness (worked out from vanilla's 3,256 records). Each record names the two "wing" particles A and B (the triangles' far corners) and the edge's C and D:

    • Weights: A and B get dB/(dA+dB) and dA/(dA+dB), where dA and dB are the wings' distances from the edge line. C and D get −(1−t) and −t, where t is where the wings' weighted point meets C–D.
    • h = the sum of weight × position. It is zero when the two triangles lie flat.
    • restCurvature = −(h·n) / (dA·dB), with n the unit normal of triangle A-C-D.
    • bendStiffness = −k / Σ(weight² · invMass), with k from 0 to 1.
    • The set's maxRestPoseHeightSq = 64 × the largest h².

    Vanilla paints k per hinge (106 of 128 cloths vary it), commonly 0.5 to 0.8. A hinge works on the four particles of its two triangles, so it doesn't matter whether its edge is also a link.

    Local range. Up to four sets per cloth, each with its own stiffness, shape and normal handling. Each record gives a maximum distance, and optionally maximum and minimum normal distances and a per-particle stiffness. Long garments rely on it to stay near the body: the Khan skirt at 0.1 m, the trench coat 0.5 m, the Varuun ambassador up to 1 m.

    Bone planes. Each record is a plane (nx, ny, nz, d) in the bone's frame, so it moves with the bone, plus a particle, the bone and a stiffness.

    • The particle is kept where n·x + d ≥ 0. All 5,421 vanilla records sit on that side at rest.
    • Vanilla uses them for:
    • hair: 2–9 planes on the head, to keep it out of the face
    • the ponytail: planes on the neck
    • skirts and coats: planes under the hips, to stop flaps flipping up
    • the Starborn cape: two hip planes per particle, a wedge that turns with the pelvis
    • No vanilla cloth uses a plane as a floor.

    5. Collision shapes

    perInstanceCollidables holds the shapes. collidableTransformMap gives the bone each one follows and its offset. Per particle, staticCollisionMasks says which shapes it collides with: bit k for the cloth's k-th shape.

    Shape Use
    hclCapsuleShape most body parts
    hclTaperedCapsuleShape skirts: a radius at each end
    hclSphereShape the Aceles only

    A mod's fox tail had leg capsules of 0.45 m on one side and 0.30–0.35 m on the other. Replacing one side with mirrored copies of the other worked fine in game.

    The most common vanilla values:

    Setting Field Vanilla
    Gravity simulationInfo.gravity (0, 0, −9.81) in 268 of 320
    Damping simulationInfo.globalDampingPerSecond 0.85–0.98
    Iterations simulate Config.numberOfSolveIterations 1 in all 320
    Sub-steps Config.subSteps 1 in 306
    Link stiffness standard and stretch 1.0 and 1.0 in 210
    Particle mass / radius / friction particleDatas 0.08 / 0.02 m / 0.5 most common
    Motion transfer transferMotionData translation 0.6–0.6 over 0–10; rotation 0–0.6 over 30–90°/s
    Transfer bone transferMotionData.transformIndex C_Head 80, C_Chest 69, C_Hips 63
    Landscape collision landscapeCollisionEnabled off in all 320

    7. In-game behaviour

    What we learned testing tails, a ponytail and a skirt:

    • Landscape collision does nothing. Turning on landscapeCollisionEnabled, at two radii, gave no ground contact on flat ground or stairs.
    • A floor is a bone plane on Root. Root stays at the feet when the actor crouches or lies down. A plane facing up about 3 cm above it, holding every free particle at stiffness 1, keeps a tail out of the ground. It's flat at the feet, so it's wrong on slopes and stairs, and it holds particle centres, not the mesh around them.
    • Stiffness is applied once per iteration (position-based dynamics). With one iteration even 0.999 barely holds; a fox tail looked right at 4 iterations and hinge stiffness 0.9.
    • Hinges alone don't hold a long strip up against gravity. Pinning more particles at the base does; so does local range, graded from small near the root to large at the tip.
    • On a strip, the rung hinges control bending and the diagonal ones control twist. To free a stretch of strip, zero them all.
    • An anchor particle off the strip's plane must use standard links. With stretch links (and the root rung unpinned) a tail hung straight down.
    • Keep the sim mesh simple. A closed triangular tube twitches and twists at any setting. Its hinges share triangles and fight each other, so stiffer hinges or more iterations make it worse. With its hinges at 0 it's calm but kinked near the body and stiff. A flat strip works well.
    • Shipping a re-exported vanilla piece needs its .mesh in Data\geometries. A skirt looked distorted until it was deployed.
